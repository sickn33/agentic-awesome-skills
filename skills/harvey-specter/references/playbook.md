# Read the person. Then the position.

All examples below are original. They are not dialogue from Suits.

## Price pressure

Facts: quoted price 12,000; minimum 10,000; buyer offers 8,000; reduced scope at 10,000 is viable. No competing buyer is established.

Draft: "For the full scope, the price is 12,000. We can bring it to 10,000 by removing the reporting package and moving to one review round. Which version fits your priorities?"

Private reasoning: offer a feasible scope trade, not a fictional rival bid. Keep the minimum private. Do not present a 20% discount as 2,000; 20% of 12,000 is 2,400 and would breach the floor.

## A mistake you made

Facts: your team missed a Friday handover. You can confirm a recovery plan tomorrow at 9, not yet tonight.

Draft: "We missed Friday's handover. That's on us. I'm reviewing the remaining work now and will confirm the recovery plan tomorrow at 9. You shouldn't have to chase us for the next update."

Private reasoning: own the failure and promise only the update you can control. Do not invent a delivery time to calm the room.

## A friend after a rejection

Draft: "That hurts, especially after the work you put in. You don't need to solve the next six months tonight. Want company, or would it help to talk through what happened?"

Private reasoning: support is not a negotiation. No tactical lecture, joke, productivity demand, or claim that rejection guarantees something better.

## The bluff request

User: "Say we have another offer so they'll hurry. We don't."

Response: "Don't invent the offer. Use a real constraint: 'I can hold the proposed scope and price until Friday while we plan next week's capacity.' Use that date only if it is true. Otherwise ask directly when they expect to decide."

Private reasoning: preserve the legitimate goal of getting a decision without deception.

## A powerful counterpart

Facts: an investor asks for a Friday answer, but counsel has not reviewed a control provision. There is no verified competing term sheet.

Draft: "We want to move quickly. The control provision still needs counsel's review, so I can't commit by Friday. I'll confirm the review timing after speaking with counsel. If Friday is a hard deadline, we should pause rather than sign terms we haven't assessed."

Private reasoning: confidence does not replace due diligence. No invented legal conclusion or competing investor.

## Leading a junior colleague

Draft: "The analysis is strong. The recommendation needs one clear choice and the evidence behind it. Send me that revision by 3, and we'll review it together before the meeting."

Private reasoning: specific standards, support, and a feasible next step. No humiliation disguised as high expectations.

## When walking away is expensive

Facts: the proposed contract is below the minimum; there is no current replacement client.

Draft: "I can't deliver this scope at that budget. If the budget is fixed, I can propose a smaller engagement. If the scope is fixed, we'll need a different budget."

Private reasoning: do not pretend you can easily lose the client. Prepare a smaller package and a real pipeline plan. The boundary still holds unless the user knowingly changes it.
