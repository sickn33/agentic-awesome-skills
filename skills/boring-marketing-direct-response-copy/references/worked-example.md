# Worked Examples: From Generic to Copy That Sells

Three fictional or sample businesses, one per kind of buyer. They show the craft contrast
only; none is proof, research or a claim the business can make. In a real draft, add
proof only when the user can substantiate it.

## SaaS: Ledgerly, an invoicing tool for independent professionals (fictional)

**Generic**

> Ledgerly is a comprehensive invoicing solution that helps independent professionals
> streamline their billing workflow with intuitive tools and efficient processes.

**Rewritten**

> Send the invoice. Stop babysitting it.
>
> You finish the work, hit send, and then the second job starts: checking whether it was
> opened, writing the polite nudge, wondering if you're being annoying.
>
> Ledgerly keeps invoices, payment links and reminders in one place. You do the work. It
> keeps the bill from becoming another task on your list.
>
> See Ledgerly →

**What changed:** a felt moment instead of category language, the scene the reader
already knows, ordinary verbs, and no invented customer count.

## Local service: Hartline Plumbing, Dallas TX (a sample business)

Traffic: a Google ad that says "Same-Day Water Heater Replacement, Dallas".

**Generic**

> Welcome to Hartline Plumbing! We are a family-owned plumbing company proudly serving the
> Dallas area with quality workmanship and exceptional customer service. Contact us today
> for all your plumbing needs!

**Rewritten**

> Same-day water heater replacement in Dallas.
>
> Your installed price in writing before we touch anything. Call by noon, hot water
> tonight.
>
> 4.9 from 312 Google reviews · Haul-away and permit included
>
> Call now, a person answers →

**What changed:** the headline repeats the ad's promise in the ad's words. The price fear gets
a specific answer instead of another "upfront pricing" claim. "Family owned" and
"quality workmanship" moved to a small trust strip, because every competitor says them.
The button says what happens when you tap it. Every line is something the sample business
already does: the ad promises same-day, the crew prices in writing before starting, and
haul-away and permit are on its price sheet. In a real job, a line with no source in the
brief, the site or the reviews comes out and goes on the To make this true list.

## Coach: an online running coach for runners over 40 (fictional)

**Generic**

> Unlock your full running potential with personalized coaching designed to help runners
> of all levels achieve their goals and crush their PRs!

**Rewritten**

> A faster 10K after 40, without another injury.
>
> Most plans are written for 25-year-olds: more miles, more speed work, and a sore knee by
> week six. This one adds two 20-minute strength sessions a week and fewer junk miles,
> because over 40 the injuries come from weak hips and calves, not from running too
> little.
>
> Do the sessions for six weeks. If your easy pace hasn't improved, you get a full refund.
>
> Start the 12-week plan →

**What changed:** the headline names the reader (over 40) and their fear (injury). The
mechanism gets a "because". The guarantee is a sentence with a number and a window. "Of
all levels" went, since a plan for everyone convinces no one.
