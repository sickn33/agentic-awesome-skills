# Local Service Copy Patterns

Load when writing for a business customers call from a map or a search: plumbers,
roofers, HVAC, electricians, dentists, med spas, cleaners, auto repair. Headline, offer,
proof and CTA patterns, plus the promise-match rules for pages that ads point at.
Examples use Hartline Plumbing, Dallas TX (hartlineplumbing.com), a sample business.
Prices are blanks or illustrative.

## The local reader

A local visitor is usually on a phone, often stressed, and decides in seconds. They're
answering four questions, in this order: Do you do this job? Here, in my area? Now, and
for roughly what? Can I trust you? Then they want the number. The first screen answers all
four and shows the number. Everything below the first screen is for the minority still
comparing, usually on planned projects.

## Promise match with the ad

The page an ad opens has to keep the ad's promise in the ad's words, on the first screen.
The rules:

1. **Same service word.** If the keyword and ad say "water heater replacement", the
   headline says "water heater replacement", not "plumbing services".
2. **Same promise.** If the ad says "same-day", the headline or subhead says "same-day",
   with its condition ("call by noon").
3. **Same offer.** If the ad says "$__ drain clearing", that price is on the first screen.
4. **Same area.** The city or neighborhood in the ad appears in the headline or eyebrow.
5. **One number.** The number on the page is the number in the ad's call extension or a
   tracking number that rings the same phone.
6. **One page per promise.** Ad groups with different promises (emergency vs replacement)
   point at different pages, or at one page with a different first screen per ad.

Check it by placing the ad and a phone screenshot of the first screen side by side. If a
stranger can't match them in five seconds, rewrite the page, not the ad. Calls-only ads,
Local Services Ads and Google Business Profile posts make promises too; the profile's
description and the landing page say the same thing.

## Headlines by lever

Name the service and the place, then one promise on one lever. Put a second lever in the
subhead. Every pattern below is a shape, not a claim: use a lever only when the business
already delivers it (the claim gate in SKILL.md). A guarantee or a time window it doesn't
keep yet goes on the To make this true list.

**Speed** (emergencies; only with a time the business keeps)
- "Water heater replaced today in Dallas. Call by noon."
- "A plumber at your door in 2 hours, 7 days a week."
- "Burst pipe? A person answers, day or night."

**Price clarity** (urgent jobs and repeat jobs)
- "Water heater replaced, $____ installed. Haul-away and permit included."
- "The price we quote is the price you pay. In writing, before we start."
- "Drain cleared for $____. If we can't clear it, you don't pay."

In a Housecall Pro survey of 1,040 US homeowners (Oct 2025, vendor data), 97% said
transparent pricing matters when hiring a pro and 77% named hidden or surprise costs as a
top frustration. When the owner won't post an exact price, use the next best: a "from"
price, a range with what moves it ("$____ to $____ depending on size and venting"), or the
process ("price in writing before we touch anything").

**Guarantee** (when the top complaint is no-shows, overruns or callbacks)
- "On time, or the trip charge is on us."
- "If the repair fails within a year, we fix it free."
- "Your water heater price in writing before we touch it."

**Proof** (planned projects, and any market where everyone claims the same thing)
- "1,200 Dallas homeowners rate us 4.9." (real count and rating only)
- "Over 3,000 water heaters installed in East Dallas since 2009." (only if counted)
- "See the camera video before you pay for anything."

**Service area** (crowded metros, and pages per town)
- "Plumbers for Lake Highlands, Lakewood and East Dallas."
- "Your East Dallas plumber. Most calls reached in under an hour." (only if measured)

What fails locally: "Your trusted plumbing experts", "Quality service you can count on",
"Serving all your plumbing needs", "Look no further". They name nothing and fit every
competitor.

## Offers

The offer often outsells the headline. Shapes that work (detail in the positioning skill's
`references/hormozi-offer.md`, local section): a flat-rate job with what's included in one
line; the trip fee credited to the repair; good, better, best options in writing;
maintenance plans; financing as a monthly number; a same-day rule with a condition. An
add-on (haul-away, the camera video, priority scheduling) says more than "$50 off" an
unposted price.

## Proof

- **Rating with the count**, near the headline: "4.9 from 1,212 Google reviews".
- **Reviews that name the before, the time and the price.** "Water heater died Sunday
  night, new one in by 10am Monday, price matched the quote." First name, last initial,
  neighborhood. Quote real reviews with the customer's permission; never write one.
- **Photos of real jobs and the real crew.** In the same Housecall Pro survey, 68% of
  homeowners said they want photos, videos or checklists showing completed work. Stock
  photos of smiling plumbers read as filler.
- **The license number and the warranty term, stated plainly.** They survive an AI
  summary; "licensed professionals" doesn't.
- **Named neighborhoods** served, in the body and the FAQ.

## The trust strip

One line under the CTA for the table stakes: licensed and insured, family owned, years in
business, background-checked techs. Small text. These claims are on every competitor's
page, so they reassure without differentiating; they never take the headline.

## CTAs

- **The call is usually the primary action.** Tap-to-call button with the number visible,
  saying what happens: "Call now, a person answers."
- **Text and online booking as second options** for people at work. In the Housecall Pro
  survey, 80% of homeowners said online booking influences who they hire.
- **Forms ask only what dispatch needs:** name, phone, zip, what's wrong. Each extra field
  costs leads.
- **Under the button,** the line that removes the live fear: "Price in writing before we
  start", "Arrival window by text", "No charge if we can't fix it".
- **After-hours honesty.** If nobody answers at night, say when someone will call back.
  Promising 24/7 and sending callers to voicemail creates the bad review.

## Length by job type

| Job type | Page shape |
|---|---|
| Emergency | One screen does it: service, area, "a person answers", rating, call button. Then a short "what happens when you call" in three steps, the area list, a few reviews. |
| Urgent, can wait a day | First screen with price or price process and arrival window. Then the method, reviews, guarantee, a short FAQ (trip fee, timing, warranty, areas). |
| Planned project | First screen with the outcome and proof. Then the problem explained, the method, photos and cases of similar jobs, warranty, financing, a longer FAQ, and a second CTA to book an estimate. |

## Local tells to cut

"Look no further", "we pride ourselves", "your one-stop shop", "for all your [trade]
needs", "top-notch", "second to none", "customer satisfaction is our top priority", "call
today!" with nothing behind it, and a hero headline that's the company name.

## Sample first screen (Hartline Plumbing, a sample business; ad: "Same-Day Water Heater Replacement, Dallas")

```
Eyebrow:   Dallas water heater replacement
Headline:  Same-day water heater replacement in Dallas.
Subhead:   Your installed price in writing before we touch anything. Call by noon, hot water tonight.
Proof:     4.9 from ____ Google reviews (real count)
Button:    Call now, a person answers  ·  (214) ___-____
Under:     Haul-away and permit included. On time, or the trip charge is on us.
Strip:     Licensed master plumbers · TX #____ · Family owned since ____
```
