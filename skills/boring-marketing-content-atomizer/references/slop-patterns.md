Enforcement context updated (LinkedIn May 20 + Jul 30, X Jul 16, Instagram Apr 30).

**When to load:** before writing any hook, CTA, or post structure in /boring-marketing-content-atomizer, and as the lint list in the Phase 4 quality gate. Companion file: brand-voice's `references/ai-tells.md` covers prose-level word/phrase tells; this file covers post-level hooks, CTAs, and structures. Lint against both.

**Why this list exists.** Every pattern below was once effective, which is how it became a pattern. By 2026 each has been generated millions of times, so it now does three kinds of damage at once. Rankers demote it (X's ranker suppresses engagement bait and combative framing and, since Jul 2026, removes repeat solicitors from its creator program; LinkedIn's May 2026 classifier confines generic-AI posts and automated comments to the author's own network, and readers can report "AI slop"). Readers pattern-match it as slop and scroll past. And it brands the account as automated. The unifying diagnostic: **every banned hook below could be written without knowing anything about the content.** That's what makes them slop: they are content-free containers. The fix is never a better formula; it's the content's own specifics (see Hook craft, bottom).

## Banned hook openers

If a draft's first line matches any of these (or a light paraphrase), rewrite from the content's specifics.

**Fake contrarianism** (claims courage while saying something safe):
- "Unpopular opinion: ..." / "Hot take: ..." / "Controversial take: ..."
- "I'm going to get hate for this but ..." / "I'll probably get cancelled for this ..."
- "This will piss off [group], but ..."
- "Harsh truth:" / "Brutal honesty:" / "Hard pill to swallow:"
- "[Popular thing] is dead. Here's what's replacing it:"
- "Stop doing [X]. It's killing your [Y]."
- "Everything you know about [X] is wrong."
- "Don't hire me if you want yes-men": the anti-brag disqualifier list that is a humblebrag in disguise

**Fake secrets** (manufactures a conspiracy where none exists):
- "The [industry] secret no one talks about:"
- "Nobody is talking about this ..." / "...that nobody tells you"
- "I can't believe [industry/brand] doesn't want you to know this"
- "What they don't teach you in school about [X]:"
- "The hidden truth behind [X]:"

**Fake credentials** (vague tenure standing in for evidence):
- "After 10 years in [industry], here's what I know for sure:"
- "I've seen 100+ [companies/profiles/campaigns]. The same mistakes keep appearing:" (banned when the number is invented or unverifiable; see credential-in-context below for the legitimate version)
- "Most people don't know this, but ..." / "95% of [group] get this wrong:"
- "Let me save you 10 years:"
- "I rejected a candidate because they did X. Here's what I learned" (and every template-copied viral skeleton with the names swapped)

**Fake drama** (narrative tension the content can't pay off):
- "Last week, I [did something]. What happened next changed everything / shocked me / changed how I think about [X]."
- "I got fired in 2019. Best thing that ever happened to me."
- "I made $0 for 2 years. Then everything changed."
- "So there I was, [scenario] ..." (when the story is mundane)
- Fake vulnerability: a confession opener ("I cried in my car after that call") that resolves into a pitch or a humble-brag.
- "I had to make the hardest decision of my career": performative vulnerability that centers the decider, not the people affected
- "I was at the airport when a stranger told me something that changed my life": the parable with a tidy business lesson
- The workaholic flex: "logged in from the hospital" / "haven't seen my kids in three weeks" (burnout as virtue)

**Assistant-register and throat-clearing:**
- "Let's talk about [X]." / "We need to talk about [X]." / "Can we talk about [X]?"
- "Here's the thing:" / "Real talk:" / "Friendly reminder:" / "PSA:"
- "Normalize [X]." / "Daily reminder that [X]."
- "Y'all I need to talk about ..." (manufactured casualness)
- "Excited to announce" / "Thrilled to share" / "Humbled and honored to announce": the announcement register; readers scroll it as corporate output
- "Here's the part most people miss" / "I'm going to state this as clearly as possible" / "Let me be clear:": 2026 openers that promise directness and deliver standard advice
- "🚀 5 things every leader should know about [X]": the emoji-led AI listicle hook
- "A thread:" / "Thread:" / "🧵" / "Let me explain 👇" / "Buckle up." and thread announcements generally. If the first post needs to announce it's a thread, the first post has no hook. The hook IS the first post; the platform shows there's more.

**Worn-out framing devices:**
- "POV: ..." (the format outlived its novelty; the words "POV" on screen now read as template)
- "Tell me you're [X] without telling me you're [X]"
- "Nobody: / Absolutely nobody: / Me: ..."
- "Let that sink in." / "Read that again." / "Sit with that for a second."
- "...and I think that says a lot." / "I said what I said."
- Open-with-a-definition: "Webster's defines [X] as ..."
- "It's not X. It's Y." as the hook line: the signature AI construction (see ai-tells), and LinkedIn's classifier is reported to key on it

## Banned CTAs and engagement bait

X demotes these algorithmically (and since Jul 2026 removes repeat solicitors from its creator program); LinkedIn, Instagram, and Threads run bait classifiers that discount single-token replies; on every platform they read as desperation. One platform-appropriate ask maximum per piece, and the best pieces need none.

- Follow-bait: "Follow @[handle] for more [topic]" / "Follow so you don't miss part 2"
- RT/share-bait: "RT the first tweet if this was useful" / "Repost to help someone in your network"
- The follow-and-RT combo close ("If this was useful: 1. Follow 2. RT")
- Comment-bait: "Comment [WORD] and I'll DM you the [resource]" (DM-gate mechanics are demoted and widely resented)
- Tag-bait: "Tag someone who needs to see this"
- Reply-bait stapled to content: "Which one hits different?" / "Thoughts?" / "Agree?" / "Am I wrong?"
- Save/send stacking: "Save this. Share it. Send it to a friend." (a genuinely useful piece earns saves without instructions)
- "Part 2?" as comment farming
- Like-gates: "Hit like if you agree" / "Like this if you've ever ..."
- Poll-bait with a fake question whose answer is the pitch
- "Bookmark this." / "(bookmark for later)"
- Carousel/thread closers that restate nothing but ask for three actions ("Found this useful? → Follow → Repost → Save")
- Vote-bait: "Like for A, comment for B" / "React with 🎉 if you agree" (simulated polls via reactions)
- Guilt-share: "Only 1% will share this" / "Share if you care about [cause]"
- "Type [emoji] for the link" / "Drop a YES below": single-token replies are discounted by every bait classifier
- "I'll follow everyone who replies" / follow-for-follow, named in X's Jul 2026 enforcement
- Engagement-bait polls: "Should you answer email within 24 hours? A) Yes B) No C) It depends"
- "Comment your [age/role] and I'll guess your [X]" (Threads)

## Banned structures

- **The TL;DR recap unit**: a thread's penultimate post or carousel's penultimate slide restating the 5 points just made, followed by the CTA unit. Both are filler; end on the strongest point instead.
- **The 3-line LinkedIn drama opener**: "I did [X]. / Everyone said [Y]. / Here's what happened:", and its cousin, one-sentence-per-line "broetry" white-space poetry throughout an entire post.
- **Colon-cliffhanger as every hook ending**: "Here's why:" / "Here's how:" / "Let me explain:" are fine occasionally and slop when they are the default hinge of every post.
- **Numbered-list threads of vague points**: "7 lessons" where each lesson is a fortune-cookie line ("4. Consistency beats intensity"). If a point has no example, number, or named mechanism, it doesn't earn a slot.
- **The fake-listicle hook**: "[N] [things] that will [outcome]. (Number [X] will surprise you.)"
- **Uniform thread rhythm**: every post in a thread shaped identically (claim, two sentences, punchline). Humans vary; templates don't.
- **Quote-graphic-of-own-post** recycling as new content.
- **The "value sandwich"**: two slices of generic insight around a pitch, on Reddit especially. The community detects it instantly and the account loses standing it can't buy back.
- **Mic-drop fragment closers on every piece** ("And that changes everything." / "Simple as that." / "That's it. That's the post.")
- **Emoji-bulleted feature lists** (🚀 ✅ 💡) in registers where the brand never uses them; see ai-tells.md.
- **Template-copied viral skeleton**: last week's viral structure with names and details swapped. Platforms fingerprint duplicated text (X reported 1.5M "stolen posts" in one cycle) and readers recognize the skeleton by line two.
- **Generic truism carousel**: 12 slides of one-line headers ("Slide 3: Be authentic. Slide 4: Build relationships"). Document posts win on LinkedIn only when the slides carry specifics.
- **Recycled quote graphic**: stock sunrise/mountain image + famous-person quote + "Read that again."
- **Repost-with-a-watermark / aggregation**: re-uploading others' content with an intro, watermark, or speed change. Instagram stopped recommending aggregator accounts (Apr 2026); X demotes watermark-reposts (Jul 2026). Material transformation (commentary, added text, voiceover) is the only version that survives.
- **Restating comments at scale**: comments that paraphrase the post back ("Great point about X!") are exactly what LinkedIn's automation classifier suppresses; a comment must add something the post didn't.

## Hook craft: first principles (the replacement)

A hook is the most interesting true thing in the piece, stated plainly and specifically. Build hooks from these principles, not formulas, because formulas are how everything above got burned. The examples below use Hartline Plumbing, Dallas TX (a sample business) or a roofer in Tampa, with illustrative numbers.

**1. Specificity is the hook.** A real number, a named thing, a concrete scene outperforms any template, because it can't be pattern-matched and it carries information. "We moved the phone number to the top of our mobile page and booked calls went from 11 a week to 19" (illustrative) needs no formula, because the specificity does all the work. Test: could this first line have been written without reading the source? If yes, it's a container, not a hook.

**2. Earned tension.** Open a gap the piece actually closes, where the tension comes from the content: a surprising result, two facts that shouldn't both be true, a cost nobody prices in. Withholding ("you won't believe what we found") is fake tension, because it taxes the reader before paying them. State the surprising thing; the reader's "wait, why?" is the tension.

**3. Credential-in-context.** Standing should be woven into the claim as checkable evidence, not announced as tenure. "After reading 40 one-star reviews of Dallas plumbers last month, 31 were about a missed arrival window" (illustrative) earns trust because it's specific enough to be falsifiable. "After 10 years in plumbing, trust me" asks for trust; the specific version produces it. Never fabricate the credential. If the source has no numbers, the hook leans on principle 1 or 2 instead.

**4. The first line survives standalone.** On every platform, the first line is read by 5-10x more people than the rest. It must deliver a complete thought (a claim someone could disagree with, repeat, or screenshot), not a teaser for one. If the first line only works as setup, the second line is usually the real hook.

**5. Front-load the best material.** The strongest stat, sharpest line, or biggest surprise goes first, not saved for a payoff. Feeds don't reward delayed gratification; nobody is "still reading" out of politeness.

**6. The deletion test.** Draft the piece, then delete the first line and reread. If it got stronger, the original first line was throat-clearing. Repeat until deleting hurts.

**7. Claim something falsifiable.** A hook a smart peer could dispute invites the replies that actually drive distribution (constructive disagreement is boosted; see platform-facts). "Quality work wins" is wallpaper: nobody can disagree, so nobody engages. "Our before-and-after photo carousels get 3x the saves of our tip posts, and I think it's because a homeowner can show one to a spouse" (illustrative) is a position.

**Worked contrast** (illustrative throughout):

| Slop | Craft |
|---|---|
| "Unpopular opinion: replacing a water heater early isn't a waste." | "A planned water heater swap in Dallas ran about $1,400 less than an emergency one across our last 40 jobs. Say $2,900 against $4,300. Most of the gap was the after-hours fee, not the unit." |
| "After 10 years in roofing, here's what I know about estimates:" | "We sent the same roof estimate two ways for 6 weeks. The plain one-page version won by a wide margin over the glossy 12-page one. The reason took us a month to accept." |
| "The Google Maps secret nobody talks about: 🧵" | "Hartline got 9 new Google reviews in 90 days. The three plumbers above it got between 31 and 52. Only one thing differed, and it wasn't the work." |
| "Stop paying for leads. It's killing your margins." | "Door-knocking after the storm closed 9 of our last 10 roofing jobs in Tampa, and it still doesn't scale. Here's where the line actually sits for us." |

## Lint procedure (Phase 4)

1. Check the first line of every piece against **Banned hook openers**. A match or near-paraphrase fails.
2. Check every ask against **Banned CTAs**. More than one ask per piece fails; any bait-pattern ask fails.
3. Check the shape against **Banned structures**: recap units, broetry, uniform thread rhythm.
4. Run brand-voice's `references/ai-tells.md` over the prose. A cluster of 3+ word/phrase tells in one piece fails.
5. Any failure means rewrite from the source's specifics using the seven principles, then re-lint. Never fix a banned hook by swapping in a different formula.
