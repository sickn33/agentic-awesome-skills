# Town page anatomy

Load before writing any page in /boring-marketing-service-area-pages Phase 4. Hartline Plumbing, Dallas TX is a sample business. Facts about Garland below are placeholders that show the shape of a sourced claim. Replace them with what you actually fetched or what the ledger says.

## 1. Answer first (40-80 words)

The block a phone visitor reads before deciding to call, and the passage an AI answer is most likely to lift. It names the service, the town, the speed and the way to reach them, plus one fact only true here.

> Hartline Plumbing repairs and replaces water heaters across Garland, usually the same day when the call comes in before noon. Most Garland homes we work in were built in the 1970s and keep the water heater in the attic, so we check the drain pan and the ceiling below on every visit. Call or text (sample number) from 7am to 7pm, seven days a week.

Weak version, for contrast: "Looking for a reliable plumber in Garland? Hartline Plumbing is proud to serve the Garland community with quality plumbing services." Every word of it would fit any town and any plumber.

## 2. The work done here

The longest section, built from the ledger rows for this town. For each job type: what usually fails, how the tech finds it, what the fix involves, and what waiting costs. This is the truth-safe substance. It is specific to the trade and true in every town, so it can run long without inventing anything.

> **Water heaters leaking in the attic.** A tank that has started to weep at the base rarely lasts another season, and in an attic the first sign is often a stain on the ceiling below. We check the drain pan, the pan's drain line and the relief valve line before quoting, because a clogged pan line turns a slow leak into a ceiling repair.

Ask the owner or a tech two questions per job type if the ledger is thin: "What do you find most often when you get there?" and "What do customers wish they'd known before calling?". Their answers are better copy than anything you can generate.

## 3. What's different about this town

Only conditions that change the work, each from a source you fetched or the owner confirmed. Where to look:

| Condition | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| Water hardness | The city's annual water quality report (in the US, the Consumer Confidence Report) |
| Permit rules for the job | The city's building or permits page |
| Age of the housing stock | The county appraisal district or census housing tables, by area |
| Freeze, flood or hail history | Local news archives, the city's emergency pages |
| Utility rebates | The gas or electric utility's rebate page |
| HOA or historic district rules | The city or HOA site |

Keep the source URL for each claim in `notes.md` so the owner can check it. The page itself carries no citation tags. If nothing fetched changes the work, this section is omitted, not padded.

## 4. Proof

- Reviews that name the town or a neighborhood, quoted verbatim, with the reviewer's first name or initials as shown and the month. Never edit a review to add the town name.
- A short job story: the problem, what was found, what was done, how long it took. Confirm the customer is fine with it being described, and leave out street names and house numbers.
- Real photos from jobs in the town, screened for faces, house numbers, plates and paperwork.

## 5. Coverage and response

Plain and checkable: "Garland is 25 minutes from our shop. Most same-day calls are booked before noon." Only the hours and emergency availability the owner confirms. A page that promises 24/7 response the crew can't deliver loses the review that follows.

## 6. Pricing

The structure, in plain words: "We charge a trip fee and quote the repair before starting." Dollar amounts only when the owner publishes them. Never a price range made up to fill a FAQ.

## 7. Questions

Only real ones. Sources: the call notes and texts, reviews, and the questions Google shows for that town's searches. A town page needs no questions section if no real questions exist.

## 8. Call to action

Tap-to-call phone number, text if they answer texts, and hours. One action, repeated after the proof section.

## The areas-we-serve hub

One page, every served town, one true line each, and a link to each town page that exists:

> **Garland.** Our busiest area after Dallas, mostly water heaters and slab leaks in 1970s homes. [Garland page]
> **Plano.** We take water heater and drain calls in south Plano. Allow about 40 minutes.

Unqualified towns live here honestly instead of on a thin page of their own.

## Filler to cut on sight

- Instructions for calling ("have your address and a description of the problem ready").
- "What to expect" paragraphs that describe booking, arrival and payment in general terms.
- Meta questions ("Do you serve Garland?", "What areas do you serve?") on the Garland page.
- Neighborhood or ZIP code lists nobody asked for.
- Landmarks, sports teams, population, founding year.
- Any paragraph that would still make sense if you swapped in a different trade.
