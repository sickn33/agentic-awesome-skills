# Returns decision frameworks

## Policy and intake record

Record merchant/channel, order and original tender, customer request, delivery
and claim dates, jurisdiction, policy version, statutory remedy, warranty/service
contract, amount previously credited, active disputes and response deadline.
Do not treat illustrative merchant policies as universal law. Verify gift/no-
receipt rules and original amount paid; a price drop does not make refunding the
original price an illicit profit. Check amounts explicitly rather than assuming
a processor always caps refunds.

## Safety and release gates before economics

1. Check exact SKU/lot against the applicable recall and product safety records.
   Segregate affected goods and follow the authorised remedy; preserve evidence.
2. Suspected counterfeits remain quarantined while authenticity is checked.
   A weight/serial discrepancy is a clue, not conclusive proof or customer intent.
3. Damaged, defective or recalled lithium batteries need a trained transport
   assessment. Do not send a generic parcel kit or return a stopped shipment to
   the customer in its unsafe packaging. Requirements depend on battery condition,
   rating, configuration, mode, route and carrier; thresholds are per applicable
   battery/cell rules, not the simple sum of all Wh in a parcel.
4. Electronics must clear activation locks through the legitimate account owner
   and have verified sanitisation before resale. Do not power on unsafe units.
5. Health goods, food and medicines need category-specific handling. Acceptance
   of a complaint/return and permission to resell are separate decisions.

## Condition grading

Define category-specific cosmetic, functional, accessory and packaging criteria.
A/B/C/D are internal grades, not universal industry certifications. Retain photos,
serial/lot and test results. Never infer an exact age or percentage capacity loss
from battery cycles alone. A sealed package can still contain the wrong item.
Grade A does not itself authorise new-condition labelling; grade D does not itself
require destruction. Repair, parts recovery, regulated recycling and disposal
must follow the product-specific release and environmental requirements.

## Incremental disposition economics

For each legally and operationally eligible route, calculate:

`expected net recovery = expected realised proceeds - incremental freight -
inspection - repair - fees - holding - expected warranty/return losses - disposal`

Use probabilities for failures/non-collection, channel eligibility and a common
valuation basis. Compare refurbishment with the best alternative recovery, not
with zero. Do not count the original product cost or common customer refund twice.
A claimed donation deduction is not proceeds: estimate only a legally usable tax
benefit net of handling, with a qualified tax owner. Do not claim fair market
value automatically for business inventory.

For a returnless decision with the same refund in both branches, request a return
when its additional net recovery is positive and all other constraints permit it.
Recalls, hazards, required evidence and customer rights can override the economic
result. Customer defect allegations without inspection remain unverified reports;
track them separately from confirmed defects.

## Fraud review and exceptions

Use proportionate approved checks: order linkage, original payment reconciliation,
serial/lot records, packing evidence, consistent inspection and an opportunity for
the customer to clarify discrepancies. Account age, return frequency, shared
address, changed wording and high spending do not establish fraud. Do not combine
correlated signals into an unvalidated 0–100 probability or turn a prior false
positive into evidence. Document decisions, review deadline and appeal route;
protect personal information and monitor disparate errors.

Mandatory legal remedies precede discretionary exception economics. Assess
contribution profit and full processing cost, not gross spending as LTV. No
blanket VIP exemption or automatic refusal of first-time customers. Condition
observations must not substitute for rights analysis.

## Vendor recovery

Estimate `(collectible credit × collection probability) - incremental claim,
freight and handling costs`, with deadlines and contract rights first. A $500
claim is not always economical: at 50% collection and $300 costs, expected net
recovery is -$50. Batch only within claim windows. Preserve inspection evidence;
excess returns over a baseline are not proof that all excess units are defective.
Any chargeback, deduction or offset needs the contractual basis and authorised
owner. Reconcile accepted credits rather than treating filed claims as cash.

## Refund and dispute reconciliation

Keep physical return tracking separate from refund execution. With an open card
dispute, have the payments owner check acquirer-specific deadlines and permitted
refund routes. Avoid duplicate credits without cancelling a valid physical return
or silently losing a consumer deadline. A customer email cannot prove a bank
withdrawal. Reconcile final settlement and all partial credits. Never present a
single network deadline as universal law.

## Capacity and quality

Forecast seasonal demand from the merchant's own history and scenarios; label
assumptions. Use cohort return rates, audited grading agreement, net recovery and
legitimate-review error rates. Do not promise unsupported January multipliers,
inspection-time guarantees or percentage reductions from a proposed intervention.
Pilot and measure improvements with safety/rights deadlines held constant.

## Primary references

- [NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/88/r2/final): current
  media sanitisation guidance; Rev. 1 was superseded. Select and verify methods
  for the medium and data sensitivity; a tool name is not proof of sanitisation.
- [PHMSA lithium battery guidance](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/lithiumbatteries):
  assess the actual shipment under the applicable rules and carrier requirements.
- [CBSA casual refunds](https://www.cbsa-asfc.gc.ca/import/courier/crig-robi-eng.html):
  personal imports are distinct from commercial claims; confirm the proper filer.
