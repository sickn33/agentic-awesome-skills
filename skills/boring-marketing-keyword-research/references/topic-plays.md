# Topic plays: from one committed topic to a coordinated set of moves

Load in Phase 4 once a family is NOW, before writing its briefs. Northpeak (scheduling software for multi-location teams) and Hartline Plumbing, Dallas TX are sample businesses. Every number below is illustrative.

## Two units, two jobs

- **The family** is how you discover and rank. Every family gets one type, which says what kind of work would win it.
- **The play** is how you execute. You build one only for the family the user commits to (one, two at most). A full play for every family is the pipeline this skill exists to replace.

## The typed menu

| Type | What wins it | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Content | An answer page or guide that covers the open questions better than what's cited today | /boring-marketing-seo-content |
| Commercial | A money page (service, product, pricing) that the results already reward | /boring-marketing-landing-page for a product or pricing page; /boring-marketing-local-seo-plan for a local service page |
| Comparison | A "best X for Y" or "X vs Y" page with an honest verdict | /boring-marketing-seo-content |
| Page generation | A set of pages from one frame ("X for [industry]", "[service] in [town]") | /boring-marketing-programmatic-seo, or /boring-marketing-service-area-pages for towns |
| Citation | Getting into the sources the AI answers already draw from (lists, threads, reference sites, press) | /boring-marketing-ai-search OPTIMIZE |

Three things cut across every type and get checked on every play: **liftable structure** (answer-first blocks, tables, truthful schema), **entity authority** (one name, consistent facts on every profile and reference source), and **freshness** (a visible date, current numbers).

## The evidence types the work

| What you observed for the topic | It points to |
|---|---|
| AI answers and AI Overviews: is it answered, and who is cited | Citation (get included) or content (out-answer them) |
| The sources the answers lean on | Citation |
| People Also Ask and the mix of result types | Content: the questions become the structure, and the winning format sets the page type |
| A page of the brand's already at position 8 to 30 | Improve that page. Fixing beats writing |
| Crawl, rendering or blocked-crawler problems | The technical fix comes first |
| Demand and difficulty | The order across everything above |

## Where the AI draws its answer decides the mix

Classify every source the observed answers cite: roundup or list, community (Reddit, forums), reference (Wikipedia, government, standards bodies), comparison or review site, product or brand page, local (profiles, directories), video. The dominant kind picks the first move, and it can overrule the search intent:

- **Roundups dominate:** earn a place on those exact lists.
- **Community threads dominate:** genuine participation where someone at the business has real standing. Never seeded questions or fake accounts.
- **Reference sources dominate:** entity work, so the facts on those sources and the brand's profiles agree.
- **The answers run on generic, copyable content:** original data, one number only this business has.
- **Product or brand pages dominate:** the hub page can win directly. This is the rare case.

A commercial question answered mostly from lists and threads needs citation earning before a product page. A strong page alone loses to the lists, because the lists are what the answer reads.

## Anatomy of a play

1. **Hub.** The anchor page for the head term: a comparison or commercial page when the intent is commercial and head demand is real, a guide when the topic is informational.
2. **Spokes.** Supporting pages, one per cluster of open questions from the coverage map (`references/coverage-map.md`), or one per place or segment where the brand is absent and a peer owns the answer. Each links to the hub. Together they build the topical authority that makes the hub citable.
3. **Citation earning.** Off-site moves aimed at the named sources the answers actually use: these lists, these threads, these reference entries, these local outlets.
4. **Structural.** Answer blocks under question headings, schema that describes the page truthfully, a visible updated date, one spelling of every entity. Nearly always present and cheap. An IndexNow ping comes after something is published, never as a first move.

## How much to invest

- **Low difficulty and real demand in either space:** the full play, all four parts.
- **High difficulty or thin demand:** a thin play, the structural fixes plus one spoke at most. Say it's thin and why, so nobody over-invests in a topic the evidence calls hard.
- **Places or segments where the brand already shows up in the answers:** defend and watch. Don't attack what you hold.

## Sequence by leverage, not by type

- When the answers draw mostly on outside sources, the citation move usually goes first. It's the fastest route into the answer.
- Then the winnable scope gaps: towns or segments where a regional peer, not a national giant, owns the answer.
- The hub anchors the cluster. Structural work runs throughout.

Write one line that says why this order, grounded in what you observed.

## Every move carries five things

| Field | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| Objective | What the move achieves, in one line |
| Targets | The actual lists, threads, towns, question clusters or pages. Never "relevant publications" |
| Evidence | The observation it rests on. A move with no observed basis is dropped or labelled a judgment call |
| Owner | The skill or the person who does it |
| Acceptance check | The observable result that proves it worked: named in the answer for these 3 questions, page indexed and ranking for its query group, listed on that roundup |

## A play has to be able to prove movement

Freeze a baseline before the first move ships: the /boring-marketing-ai-search question list and its answer states, the positions for the play's terms (one batch check from the right location), and Search Console clicks and impressions for the pages involved. Each move then walks one track: planned, briefed, drafted, shipped, watching, then won or missed. Check at 2, 4 and 8 weeks after it ships. A shipped page you can find with a crawl or a site search flips to watching on its own, so no publishing integration is needed. A play with no baseline is a plan that can't tell you whether it worked.

## Worked example: Northpeak (sample business, illustrative)

**Committed family:** "shift scheduling for multi-location teams". Search demand is modest, AI-native questions are plentiful ("what's the best way to schedule staff across 6 restaurant locations?"), and difficulty reads low.

**What the observed answers lean on:** 9 of 12 cited sources are roundups ("best employee scheduling software 2026"), 2 are Reddit threads where restaurant managers compare tools, 1 is a competitor's product page. Northpeak is named in 1 of 12 observed answers, and its own site is never a source.

**Play (full, because difficulty is low and AI-native demand is real):**

| Order | Part | Move | Targets | Acceptance check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Citation | Get onto the roundups the answers cite | The 4 roundups cited most often, each with its editor and the proof to send (a real customer count, a dated feature comparison) | Listed on 2 of the 4 within 8 weeks |
| 2 | Citation | Original data | "Schedule changes per location per week", from Northpeak's own anonymized usage data | Cited in at least 1 observed answer within 8 weeks |
| 3 | Hub | Comparison page | "Shift scheduling software for multi-location teams: an honest comparison", with a verdict per team type | Indexed, page one for the query group from the target location |
| 4 | Spokes | Answer pages | 3 question clusters from the coverage map: cross-location shift swaps, labor-law rules by state, scheduling a new location's first month | Each named or cited in its cluster's answers |
| 5 | Structural | Throughout | Answer blocks, Organization and SoftwareApplication markup that matches the page, a visible updated date | Present on every page in the play |

**Why this order:** the answers read roundups, not product pages, so earning the lists moves the answer fastest. The data asset gives the roundups and the answers a number only Northpeak has.

## The local version: Hartline Plumbing (sample business, illustrative)

**Committed family:** water heater repair and replacement in Dallas and nearby towns.

**What the observed answers lean on:** the Google profile and its reviews, two "best plumbers in Dallas" lists, a neighborhood forum. The business is named for Dallas questions and absent for Garland and Richardson, where two regional plumbers are named instead.

**Play:** profile and review fixes first (/boring-marketing-google-business-profile, /boring-marketing-reviews), a placement plan for the two lists, the water heater parent page as the hub, Garland as the first town spoke because 31 jobs and four reviews there give it real proof (/boring-marketing-service-area-pages), Richardson held until the proof exists, and a plain facts block on the about page. The scope-shaped gap (present at home, absent in two towns owned by regional peers) is the sharpest kind there is, because it names both the opportunity and where to build.
