# Market Sophistication (Schwartz)

Load in Phase 4 when the stage signals conflict or the user disputes the call. From
Eugene Schwartz's "Breakthrough Advertising": copy can't create desire, it can only
channel desire that already exists, and the angle that worked when a market was new fails
once buyers have heard every claim.

## Two dials, kept apart

- **Awareness** is about the buyer: do they know the problem, that solutions exist, that
  this product exists? Copy uses it to pick the lead (`/boring-marketing-direct-response-copy`).
- **Sophistication** is about the market: how many similar claims has this buyer already
  heard? Positioning uses it to pick the angle type.

## The five stages

**Stage 1, pioneer.** First to make the claim. Buyers say "I didn't know that was
possible." Angle: state the promise plainly. Examples: the first tool to do a job that
used to need a person; trenchless sewer repair before anyone in town had the rig.

**Stage 2, enlarger.** Competitors arrived with the same promise and the claims escalate:
"fastest", "#1 rated", bigger numbers. Angle: be more specific and quantified than the
superlatives ("first invoice sent in 4 minutes", "at your door in 2 hours, 7 days a week").

**Stage 3, mechanism.** Claims have been inflated past belief. Signals: "I've tried things
like this", competitor ads you can't tell apart, reviews saying "they all promise that".
Angle: the named method that shows how the promise is kept (`unique-mechanism.md`). Most
mature software categories, most coaching niches and most local trades sit here.

**Stage 4, mechanism enlarger.** Competing mechanisms, each claiming to be better. Ads lead
with studies, numbers, photo reports, posted prices, guarantees with terms. Buyers
research heavily. Angle: proof density, a more specific or more visible mechanism.

**Stage 5, identity.** Everything has been claimed and proven. One known brand wins by
default. Angle: who the buyer is ("for people who..."), or an enemy the default brand
embodies (the upsell, the contract, the call center, the cookie-cutter plan).

## How to call the stage

1. **Paid hooks first.** Homepages lag the market by a year or more; ads reflect what's
   being said this month.
2. **The complaint list.** Claims-fatigue shows up as "they all say..." and "I've tried".
3. **Proof on the first screen.** Count posted prices, numbered guarantees, named methods
   and studies on the top competitors' first screens. None suggests Stage 2-3; several
   suggests Stage 4.
4. **The AI answers.** If assistants already compare mechanisms and cite proof for the
   category, call it Stage 3-4 however young the category feels. They list every
   competitor's claim in one answer, so buyers reach claims-fatigue in one query.

Stages vary by segment in the same market. A local plumber's emergency drain work can be
Stage 2 while water heater replacement is Stage 3 and repipes are Stage 4; a software
category can be Stage 4 for enterprise buyers and Stage 2 for a niche nobody serves. Call
the stage for the segment being positioned.

## Moving the market

A business can reset the stage by introducing something the market hasn't seen (a
mechanism moves a Stage 2 market to 3) or by narrowing the category until it is new
("bookkeeping for Shopify sellers with inventory" is younger than "bookkeeping";
"trenchless sewer repair for slab homes" is younger than "sewer repair"). The first to
name the method in a market usually owns it.
