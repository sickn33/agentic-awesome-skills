# Offers and Guarantees

Load in Phase 3 when building the offer and guarantee, or when the angle is fine and
people still don't buy (that's usually an offer problem). The frame is Alex Hormozi's
from "$100M Offers". Every price and number below is illustrative; use the business's
real ones or leave a blank for the owner.

## The value equation

Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood) / (Time Delay x Effort and Sacrifice)

| Lever | SaaS (invoicing app) | Coach (running program) | Local (plumber) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | "Get paid without chasing anyone" | "A faster 10K after 40" | "Hot water back tonight" |
| Perceived likelihood | named customers, median days-to-paid | before-and-after times, a plan built for their age | review count, photos, a written guarantee |
| Time delay | first invoice sent in minutes | a measurable change by week 6 | a real arrival window |
| Effort and sacrifice | import clients from a spreadsheet in one click | 20-minute sessions, no gym | no day off work, cleanup and haul-away included |

Most offers lose on the bottom of the equation. The product works; the setup, the wait
and the hassle are what buyers complain about. Shrinking the denominator is usually
cheaper than raising the numerator.

## Building the offer

1. **Name the dream outcome** in the customer's words.
2. **List every obstacle** between now and that outcome: "I don't have time", "I tried
   before and it failed", "I don't trust the price", "I'll have to learn a new tool".
3. **Turn each obstacle into a piece of the offer.** "No time" becomes done-for-you setup.
   "Tried before" becomes the named method and a guarantee. "Don't trust the price"
   becomes a posted or fixed price.
4. **Pick the delivery vehicle:** do-it-yourself, done-with-you or done-for-you; one-to-one,
   group or self-serve. Each level is a price tier.
5. **Trim and stack.** Keep high-value, low-cost pieces. Cut anything that adds cost without
   removing an obstacle.

## Offer shapes by business type

- **Software:** a free trial or pilot with done-for-you setup; annual plan priced as
  months free; migration included; a founding price with a stated reason.
- **Courses, coaching, creators:** a named program with a fixed length; a pay-in-full price
  beside a payment plan; bonuses that each remove one objection (a quick-start plan for
  "no time", templates for "don't know where to start"); a cohort start date as honest
  urgency.
- **Agencies and freelancers:** a paid diagnostic or audit credited to the first month; a
  fixed-scope sprint before a retainer; a pilot with a defined deliverable and an exit.
- **Local trades:** see the local section below.

## Guarantees

The grammar: the number, the window, and what happens if you miss. Then one line on what
is and isn't covered, then the terms without weasel words. "Satisfaction guaranteed"
guarantees nothing.

| Type | Example wording |
|---|---|
| Unconditional | "30 days, any reason, full refund. Keep the templates." |
| Conditional | "Do the sessions for 6 weeks. If your easy pace hasn't improved, full refund." |
| Outcome-based | "If your books don't reconcile within 60 days, we work free until they do." |
| Service | "If we miss the deadline, that month is free." |

Before writing one, ask the owner for the numbers that set what's affordable: refund rate,
missed deadlines, callbacks, churn in the first 90 days. A guarantee the business can't
keep becomes a bad review with the guarantee quoted in it. Stacking works when each
guarantee answers a different fear; three or more reads like a gimmick on a small price.

A guarantee the business doesn't offer today never goes in shipped copy, however well it
answers the complaint. Write it under **To make this true** with the numbers to check and
who signs off. The same holds for any offer piece (a bonus, a faster window, a feature)
the business can't deliver yet.

## Naming the offer (MAGIC)

Magnet (a reason to act now), Avatar (who it's for), Goal (the outcome), Interval (a
timeframe), Container (the format). "The 12-Week Over-40 10K Plan." "The 30-Day Catch-Up
Close." "The Same-Day Water Heater Swap." Skip alliteration that sounds like a gimmick to
the audience.

## Bonuses, not discounts

A discount says the price is negotiable. A bonus keeps the price and adds a reason. Each
bonus should remove a named objection; a bonus that removes "I won't have time" is worth
three that add more content. Give each a name and a defensible value (a price you charge
for it, or the cost of the nearest alternative).

## Market before offer

A mediocre offer to a starving crowd beats a great offer to an indifferent market. Check
three things: real pain, the ability to pay, and a way to reach them. Narrow the market
until all three are obvious: "bookkeeping for Shopify sellers doing $1-10M" over "small
business bookkeeping"; for a local business, the job type plus the area the crew can
reach.

## Local service businesses

For trades and other businesses customers call from a map, the offer often outsells the
angle. Offer shapes that work:

- **Flat-rate job.** One installed price for a defined, repeatable job ("50-gallon gas
  water heater, installed, haul-away and permit included, $____"). List what's included in
  one line, or the flat rate becomes the next surprise bill.
- **Trip fee credited to the repair.** "$__ to diagnose, credited in full if we do the
  work." Honest about the call-out cost and it filters tire-kickers.
- **Good, better, best** on every quote (repair, replace standard, replace upgraded), in
  writing, with each price. The customer chooses instead of deciding yes or no.
- **Maintenance plan.** Yearly tune-ups, priority scheduling and a repair discount for a
  monthly fee. It gives a reason for seasonal reminder emails.
- **Financing as a monthly number** beside the project price. State the lender's terms
  honestly; never invent an APR.
- **A same-day rule with a condition.** "Call by noon, fixed today" is more believable
  than "same-day service". Keep it only if dispatch can.
- **Add-ons over discounts.** "$50 off" an unposted price is the most saturated local ad.
  Haul-away, a camera video or a year of priority scheduling says more.

Guarantees that fit trades, each answering the fear it names:

| Type | The fear | Example wording |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival window | "They'll show up at 4 for an 8-12 window" | "If we're more than 30 minutes past your window, the trip charge is on us." |
| Price lock | "The bill will be double the quote" | "The price on your quote is the price on your invoice. Anything new gets a new quote first, in writing." |
| Workmanship | "It'll break again next month" | "If our repair fails within 12 months, we come back and fix it free, parts and labor." |
| Clean home | "They'll wreck the house" | "Shoe covers on, floors covered, everything we touched cleaned up, or $__ off." |
| No fix, no fee | "I'll pay and it still won't work" | "If we can't fix it, you don't pay for the visit." |

Three questions for the owner before any of these: how often did you miss an arrival
window last month, how often did a final bill run over the quote, and how many jobs
needed a callback?

## The check before launch

1. Is the after-state named in the customer's words?
2. Does the offer answer the top complaint from the research?
3. Can the business deliver it in its worst week?
4. Is the price, or how the price is decided, easy to find?
5. Does the guarantee have a number, a window and a consequence the owner would sign?
