# Finding Angles: the Fact-Sheet Interview and the Masters' Methods

Load in Phase 1 for the owner or founder interview, and in Phase 5 when generation feels
thin. The classic copywriters agreed on one thing: angles are found in facts, not invented
in brainstorms. The facts live in the founder's head, the delivery team's habits, the
support inbox and the reviews.

## The fact-sheet interview (Halbert)

Gary Halbert filled pages with facts about a product before writing a headline. Run a
20-minute interview with whoever builds or delivers the thing, and read the support
tickets or job history if they'll share them. Write every answer down, especially the ones
that sound boring.

**The work**
- Walk me through delivery from the first contact to the result.
- What do you do every time that the customer never sees?
- What do you measure, check or prepare before you start?
- What do you refuse to do that competitors do?
- What did you change after a refund, a churned customer or a bad review?

**The customers**
- Which customers get the best results with the least hand-holding? What do they share?
- What do buyers say on the first call or in the first email? What did they try first?
- What do customers thank you for that surprised you?
- What does a customer tell a friend after it works (that sentence is often a headline)?

**The numbers** (real numbers only; never invent)
- Time to first result, median result, refund or churn rate.
- For services: on-time rate, how often the final bill differs from the quote, callbacks.
- Review count and rating; how many reviews mention a person by name.

**The history**
- Why did you start? What did your old job, old boss or the last vendor do that you swore
  you wouldn't?

Every fact becomes one or more benefits. "Car weighs 4,000 lbs" became "safer" and
"smoother ride" for Halbert; "stocked trucks" becomes "fixed on the first visit" and "no
second day off work". The angle is usually the fact the owner thinks is too obvious to
mention.

## Kennedy's message-to-market questions

Dan Kennedy: match the right message to the right market and the copy barely has to be
good. Answer these for the segment being positioned:

1. What keeps them up at night about this problem?
2. What are they afraid will happen if they buy (wasted money, wasted time, looking
   foolish, a stranger in the house)?
3. What are they angry about, and at whom (the last vendor, the tool that didn't work)?
4. What are they embarrassed about?
5. What do they secretly want most?
6. What words do they use for the problem? Their phrase, not the industry's.
7. Who sold them something similar before and let them down, and how?

## The other masters, one move each

- **Schwartz.** Channel a desire that already exists; see `schwartz-sophistication.md`.
- **Ogilvy.** Specific detail beats adjectives. Research every ad the category has run,
  and write many headlines before picking one.
- **Hopkins.** Explain the ordinary fact nobody explains (the Schlitz move), and test with
  tracked responses rather than opinions.
- **Bencivenga.** Problem + promise + proof + proposition. If one is weak the angle
  wobbles; most first drafts lack the proposition (the offer).
- **Caples.** Self-interest first. The buyer's result belongs in the headline, not the
  company's history.

## Local service businesses

The interview works best with the owner and a lead tech together, at the shop or in the
truck. Add these questions:

- What's on your truck that most trucks don't carry?
- What do you photograph or measure before you quote? Does the customer get a copy?
- Typical arrival time after a call, and how often do you miss the window?
- Which neighborhoods or kinds of houses do you know better than anyone?
- What do customers say to their spouse after you leave?

And add the fears specific to letting a stranger into the house: the surprise bill, the
no-show, the upsell, the mess. One of them is usually the angle.

## Synthesis

1. Interview and read the reviews (facts).
2. Read competitors' first screens, ads and bad reviews (the landscape).
3. Call the stage for the segment (Schwartz).
4. Find the ordinary fact nobody explains (Hopkins, `unique-mechanism.md`).
5. Build the offer and the guarantee (`hormozi-offer.md`).
6. Test with tracked responses, not opinions.
