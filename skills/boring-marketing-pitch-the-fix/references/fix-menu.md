# The fix menu

Load at Phase 6, or whenever a prospect asks "what else do you do?". Fourteen fixes an agency can sell one at a time. Each names the broken thing the way an owner would, the fix, how you'll both know it worked, and how long that takes to show.

**Prices are yours.** Example only, replace before anything goes out: a single fix like these might be offered at a fixed price in a range such as $500 to $1,500, depending on scope. That range is an illustration, not a market rate or a recommendation.

The rule for every row: agree the baseline before you touch anything. A fix with no "before" number can't show it worked.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "People can't find our number on a phone" | A tap-to-call bar and the number at the top of every page | Calls from the site (a tracking number on the site, or a call-click event) for two weeks before and after | 2 to 4 weeks | `/boring-marketing-first-screen-check`, `/boring-marketing-landing-page` |

## Choosing the first fix

- The first fix is the one in the email. Don't swap it after they say yes.
- Rows 1 to 6 show results fastest because they sit next to the phone. They make the best first fixes.
- Rows 13 and 14 are real but slow to prove. Sell them second, once a fast fix has earned trust.
- A row the business can't support (row 6 for a shop that won't answer at night) isn't a fix. Say so, and pick another.

## The proof report

One short note at the end of the window: the baseline, the new number, the source, and one sentence on what else changed in that time (a storm, a price change, a new truck). An honest "it didn't move" with a reason keeps the client longer than a vague win.
