# Logistics exception edge cases

These are hypothetical operational patterns, not documented incidents involving named companies or proof of legal responsibility. Follow the workflow and deadline checks in the root skill and decision framework. Safety response comes before documentation; no external action is authorized by a scenario.

## 1. Pharma temperature excursion with conflicting readings

Quarantine under the receiving site's approved procedure and preserve custody. Compare calibrated product loggers, sensor positions, reefer supply/return readings, door events and loading records on one timezone-aware timeline. Different sensors measure different locations; a slope or delay alone cannot diagnose compressor failure or allocate fault. Ask qualified quality personnel and the manufacturer for product-, presentation- and lot-specific stability assessment. Never infer that insulin is usable from a generic temperature/time cutoff, or release/destroy stock on model advice. Arrange approved replacement options in parallel with the claim.

## 2. Damage discovered during consignee unloading

Preserve the signed receipts, actual observed condition, origin images and authorized dock footage. Obtain independent contemporaneous statements without coaching witnesses. A signature, refusal, forklift pattern or missing footage is not conclusive proof of fault or fraud. Assess safe storage and approved partial replacement without coercing acceptance. Send protective notices and arrange a compliant monetary claim when required; an unspecified “under investigation” note may not preserve rights.

## 3. High-value shipment without scans

Distinguish last electronic event from last verified physical location. Through verified carrier contacts request trailer/custody records and a physical search; a trailer arrival does not prove its cargo arrived. Missing scans or a disrupted terminal neither establish nor rule out theft. Escalate based on safety and time sensitivity rather than waiting a fixed 48–72 hours. Seek confirmed replacement availability without inventing a manufacturer's loaner program or assuming expedite costs are recoverable.

## 4. Cross-border customs hold

Separate tariff classification, quantity, valuation, origin and permit questions. Compare commercial invoice, physical count, packing/tally and actual customs hold notice. An intact seal does not prove the original count. Ask a licensed local specialist to determine the current commodity classification and lawful correction process; do not reuse illustrative Mexican fractions or assume USMCA eligibility. Correct documents truthfully with an audit trail and authorized filing. Request itemized broker/detention charges; cost allocation and release depend on documents and local procedure.

## 5. Partial deliveries with a SKU discrepancy

Reconcile quantities by SKU for the master BOL, every drop and any remaining terminal stock. For example, a hypothetical 22-pallet shipment split 10 + 8 + 4 totals 22; 8 + 8 + 4 totals only 20 and must not be treated as fully delivered. Equal total pallet counts can conceal the wrong SKU mix but do not prove a swap. Investigate origin, terminal and site records. Arrange an approved exchange or replacement while preserving deadlines and documenting authorized retrieval of overage goods.

## 6. Team-driver paperwork gap

Collect each actual delivery receipt, consignee WMS receipt, driver statement and authorized custody records. Do not forge or backdate the missing signature. A generic receipt may have evidentiary value; its absence does not automatically concede liability. ELD stop duration cannot establish the number of pallets unloaded, and a late claim is not proof that loss occurred at the consignee. Reconcile independent evidence and submit the dispute to the authorized claims owner.

## 7. Concealed intermodal damage

For leaking chemicals or aerosols, stop unsafe work and involve trained responders before inspection. Preserve packaging, damage position, seal and interchange records when safe. Review the through BOL and applicable legal regimes with counsel/insurer; do not assume each domestic leg is governed by Carmack. Send prompt protective notices to appropriate parties, then compliant claims without duplicate recovery. No three-day concealed-damage rule comes from Carmack, and an intact seal does not eliminate tampering or pre-loading defects.

## 8. Broker insolvency and cargo-release payment demand

Verify authority and insurer/surety details independently; inactive authority alone does not prove bankruptcy. Confirm any court proceeding from official records. Involve legal, finance and procurement immediately while planning safe product preservation. Carrier liens, direct-payment exposure, broker debts and bankruptcy rights depend on law and agreements; 49 USC 14103 is not a general cargo-lien statute. Do not wire money, guarantee debts or negotiate a release autonomously. A “paid under protest” statement does not guarantee recovery or prevent double payment. Use independently verified payment instructions and an approved written arrangement; ask the qualified owner whether bond/trust or estate claims apply and calendar their actual deadlines.

## 9. Weather emergency surcharge dispute

Preserve the governing contract, incorporated tariff version, notice, effective date and shipment-level invoices. Separate already tendered shipments from future tenders and compare precedence clauses and geographical scope. Weather confirmation alone does not establish force majeure or entitlement to a surcharge. Request an itemized explanation, approved alternate quotes and legal assessment; do not refuse freight payment, unilaterally debit invoices or promise volume changes. Document the agreed rate, dates, credits and preserved rights.

## 10. Repeated shortages through one terminal

Use consistent definitions and matching observation periods for affected and comparison shipments. Fourteen incidents across a 90-day period cannot be divided by a single month's 120 loads and called the 90-day rate. Account for routed volume, product mix, missing data and other custody points; correlation is not proof of organized theft or statistical significance. Preserve serial/tamper records and refer evidence privately to authorized security personnel. Do not accuse individual workers, deploy covert bait trackers, contact employers or conduct surveillance from this playbook. Keep separate claims timely; an aggregate summary need not replace valid individual claims.

## 11. Hazardous-material release

Withdraw from exposure using the site/emergency plan and contact emergency responders. Do not touch leaking containers, collect close-up photographs, select PPE, neutralize chemicals or attempt cleanup from model instructions. Qualified responders determine isolation, containment, medical assessment and further transport. Authorized compliance personnel must check reporting triggers under current rules and the actual substance, concentration, quantity and circumstances. Do not use a generic gallon threshold or an assumed 100-lb chemical RQ for a solution.

[PHMSA incident-reporting guidance](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/standards-rulemaking/hazmat/frequently-asked-questions-hazardous-materials-incident-reporting) distinguishes telephone reporting under 49 CFR 171.15 from detailed reports under 171.16. For qualifying incidents, telephone notice is as soon as practical and no later than 12 hours; detailed reporting is generally within 30 days of discovery, subject to applicability and exceptions. These outside limits are not permission to delay emergency response; other state/environmental duties may differ. Preserve the SDS, shipping papers, qualified scene records and notification confirmations. Do not derive container fill limits, securement compliance or final liability from the scenario.

## 12. JIT deadline not reflected in carrier paperwork

Compare the customer's actual scheduling commitment with accepted carrier instructions, rate confirmation, BOL, notices and established course of dealing. Missing a time window from one document does not establish zero liability, and a customer chargeback does not prove recoverable consequential damages. Obtain the supported downtime calculation and counsel's causation/notice assessment. Do not admit liability, accept a chargeback or offer a fixed percentage settlement without authority. Correct future tender/acknowledgment controls and assess approved buffers and backup capacity using actual operational constraints.

## Evidence and handoff

For every scenario provide verified facts and provenance, disputed/missing facts, immediate safety controls, recovery options and costs, deadlines with sources, decision owner, draft communications and the next checkpoint. Keep sensitive personal, medical, security and payment material in restricted systems. No guaranteed recovery, timing, clinical disposition or legal outcome follows from these examples.
