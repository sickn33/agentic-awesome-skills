# The Named Method (Unique Mechanism)

Load in Phase 3 when the mechanism is weak or generic, or when the owner says "we don't
really do anything different". From Todd Brown and the direct-response tradition: the
mechanism is the HOW behind the promise. It answers "why would this time be different?"

**The claim gate applies.** A mechanism names something the business does today, found
in the brief, the site, the reviews or the owner interview. Never reverse-engineer one
from how the product might work ("a usage-signal system that knows when you're low")
when nothing says it exists. If the facts hold no step worth naming, ship the angle
without a method and put the proposed one under **To make this true** with what it would
take to make it real.

## Why it matters

In a crowded market everyone makes the same promise, and the buyer has tried things that
made it before. A promise alone gets "they all say that". A mechanism gives the buyer a
reason to believe the result, because the way it works is different and they can see it.

## The three kinds

**1. Named, existing.** The business already does something specific and never named it:
the camera inspection before every sewer quote, the weekly video check-in in a coaching
program, the reconciliation step a bookkeeper runs before closing the month. Name it and
it becomes a method.

**2. Unspoken, revealed.** Every competent provider does it and nobody explains it. Claude
Hopkins made Schlitz famous by describing the steam-cleaned bottles every brewery used.
The one who explains it first owns it. This is the richest vein in mature markets.

**3. New.** A genuine innovation: original research, a new process, equipment or software
competitors don't have. Rare and strong. Document it and put it first. It counts only
once it's live; a planned feature is a To make this true item.

## Finding it: questions for whoever does the work

- Walk me through delivery from the first contact to the result. What happens first?
- What do you check, measure or prepare that the customer never sees?
- What do cheaper competitors skip, and what goes wrong when they skip it?
- What did you change after a bad review, a refund or a churned customer?
- What's the counterintuitive step, the one people are surprised you do?

The answer usually arrives casually, because the owner thinks it's obvious.

## Examples (illustrative)

| Business | Ordinary fact | Named method | The "because" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bookkeeping for online sellers | Reconcile cost of goods to stock counts | Inventory-Aware Books | Profit on paper only matches the bank when COGS matches the shelf. |
| Invoicing SaaS | Automatic reminders on day 7 and 14 | The Two-Nudge Rule | Most late invoices are forgotten, not disputed, and a second reminder catches them. |
| Running coach | Two short strength sessions a week | The Two-Session Strength Block | Over 40, injuries come from weak hips and calves, not from running too little. |
| Agency | A call-tracking review before touching ads | The Missed-Call Audit | More ad budget is wasted while calls the ads already produce go unanswered. |
| Plumber | Camera down the drain before quoting | The Video-First Drain Check | You see the clog or the break yourself before you pay, so nobody sells you a repipe you don't need. |
| Roofer | Photos of decking, flashing and vents | The 40-Photo Roof Report | The quote matches the roof you have, not a guess from the driveway. |

## Naming it

Good names sound specific, belong to the business and hint at the benefit: a number, a
step or the result ("the Two-Nudge Rule", "the 21-Point Spring AC Check"). Weak names:
"our proven process", "the [Brand] difference", "a holistic approach".

## Explaining it

Two sentences with a "because": "We send you the camera video before we quote, because a
slow drain can be a $200 clog or a $6,000 broken line, and you should see which one before
anyone charges you."

## Tests

- **Believability.** Does it make the promise more believable? If not, it's decoration.
- **Differentiation.** Could a competitor say the same sentence? If yes, get more specific.
- **Visibility.** Can the buyer see it happen (a report, a video, a dashboard, a check-in)?
- **Explanation.** Two sentences, no jargon.
- **The "oh, that's different" test.** When prospects hear it, do they say "I haven't tried
  that" or "I've heard that before"?

## Mistakes

- A credential presented as a method ("licensed master plumber", "certified coach").
- A name with no explanation behind it.
- A difference that doesn't change the result.
- A claimed method the team doesn't follow every time. The first customer who doesn't get
  the video, the report or the check-in turns it into a complaint.
