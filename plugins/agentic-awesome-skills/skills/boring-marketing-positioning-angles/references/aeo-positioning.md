# The AI-Answer Check for Positioning

last-verified: 2026-11-13 · ttl-days: 90 · refresh: check which AI assistants and AI search surfaces buyers use now (web-search "AI search usage share [current month year]") and whether the prompts below still return named businesses with sources; update the assistant list and prompts.

Load during Phase 2, before generating angles. Skip it only when buyers genuinely never ask
an assistant before buying (an emergency plumber's burst-pipe caller doesn't). This is a
quick read for positioning; the full audit, tracking and fixes belong to `/boring-marketing-ai-search`.

## Why it matters

Buyers increasingly ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Google's AI answers before they
visit any site. The answer names a few options and gives each a short descriptor ("known
for ease of use", "good for insurance claims"). That descriptor is a position the business
didn't choose, and the attributes the answer compares on are the axes buyers will use.

## The prompts

Run each on at least two assistants you can reach. If you can't query one, ask the user to
paste its answer. Substitute the real category, audience, city and brand.

**Category:** "What's the best [category] for [audience]?" · "How do I choose a
[category]?" · "[category] vs [adjacent category]: which do I need?"

**Comparison:** "Alternatives to [leader]" · "[Competitor A] vs [Competitor B] for
[audience]"

**Brand:** "Is [brand] good? Pros and cons?" · "What is [brand] and who is it for?"

**Objections:** "What are the downsides of [category]?" · "Is [category] worth it for
[audience]?"

**Local businesses:** "Who is the best [service] company in [city]?" · "[service] in
[neighborhood]: who do people recommend?" · "How do I choose a [trade] in [city]? What
should I watch out for?"

## What to record

- **Who's named, and in what order.** The first name carries default weight.
- **The descriptor for each, verbatim.**
- **The comparison axes** the answer uses (price, ease, support, response time, warranty).
- **Whether the brand appears**, with what descriptor or caveat.
- **The sources cited.** When the assistant's view of a business comes from two old Reddit
  threads or one directory listing, say so.
- **The warnings it volunteers** ("watch for upselling", "check the cancellation terms").
  Those are live objections the angle and guarantee should answer.

Summarize in one line for the landscape:
`AI consensus: [category] framed by [axes]; named = [brands in order]; our descriptor =
"[verbatim or ABSENT]"; warnings = [list]; blind spots = [list].`

## Positioning moves

- **Don't fight the consensus head-on.** If every answer calls the leader "the easiest",
  an "even easier" angle asks buyers to overrule their assistant. Own an axis the answers
  leave out.
- **Say the descriptor you want, the same way, everywhere.** Assistants repeat what a
  business consistently says about itself across its site, profiles and the pages that
  mention it. A positioning sentence the site never states verbatim won't become the
  descriptor. Hand the exact sentence to `/boring-marketing-ai-search` and `/boring-marketing-seo-content` (for a local
  business, also `/boring-marketing-google-business-profile` and `/boring-marketing-service-area-pages`).
- **Absent from the answer?** Specificity angles do better than superiority angles.
  Assistants readily name an option for a narrow question when the broad one is locked.
- **Wrong or outdated claim?** Treat it as a live objection the copy must answer;
  `/boring-marketing-ai-search` handles the fix.

Label what you found `Data: LIVE (n prompts, n assistants, <date>)`. Answers change from
run to run, so one run is a snapshot, not a ranking.
