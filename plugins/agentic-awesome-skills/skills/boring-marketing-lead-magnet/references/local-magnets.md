# Lead Magnets for Local Service Businesses

Load in Phase 1 when the business is one customers call from a map or a search: plumbers,
roofers, HVAC, electricians, dentists, med spas, cleaners, auto repair. Examples use
Hartline Plumbing, Dallas TX (hartlineplumbing.com), a sample business. Prices are blanks.

## What changes for local

- **The goal is a booked job, not a list.** A homeowner rarely wants a newsletter from a
  plumber. The magnet is a reason to hand over a phone number at the moment they're
  deciding, so the "conversion" is a call, a booking or a quote request.
- **It answers the question asked right before buying.** What will this cost? Repair or
  replace? Is it urgent? Can I afford it? A magnet about anything else attracts browsers.
- **Their house, not a generic answer.** Inputs about their system, size, age and zip make
  the result personal, and personal is what a search result or an AI answer can't give.
- **Capture the phone and the zip.** The zip checks the service area; the phone is how the
  job gets booked. Email is optional.
- **Speed of follow-up beats any nurture sequence.** Someone who just priced a water heater
  is shopping today. A call or text within minutes matters more than email 4 of a welcome
  series (`/boring-marketing-speed-to-lead` sets that up).
- **Honest numbers only.** Every price, range and threshold comes from the owner's real
  jobs and the lender's real terms. Never invent them.

## The four that work

### 1. Instant quote or price calculator

- **Inputs:** the service, the size or type (40 or 50 gallon, gas or electric, tank or
  tankless), access (attic, garage, closet), zip, and how soon.
- **Output:** a price range with what moves it inside the range, what's included, and the
  next step: "Get your exact price in writing" or "Book a time".
- **Where the numbers come from:** the owner's invoices for that job over the last year.
  A sensible range is the middle half of what customers actually paid (drop the cheapest
  and most expensive quarter), stated as "most Dallas homes pay $____ to $____".
- **Gate or not:** default is to show the range without a gate and ask for the phone to
  lock in a time or a written quote. Hiding a range behind a form reads as the opposite of
  price transparency. Gate it when the owner wants every price check as a lead and accepts
  fewer completions; say which you chose and why.
- **Bridge:** the range builds trust; the written quote and the booking are the business.

### 2. "Repair or replace?" checker

- **Inputs:** the system (water heater, AC, furnace, roof), its age, the symptom, the
  repair quote if they have one, and recent repairs.
- **Output:** repair, replace, or borderline, with the reasoning in two sentences and what
  to ask the tech.
- **The rule:** use the owner's own rule of thumb, stated plainly. Some HVAC shops use the
  "$5,000 rule" (the unit's age times the repair cost; above 5,000, consider replacing).
  Let the owner set the thresholds and say they're a guide, not a diagnosis.
- **Bridge:** "borderline" and "replace" results lead to a free second opinion or a
  replacement quote; "repair" results build trust that pays off at replacement time.
- **Honesty:** never tell someone to replace a system the inputs say is fine. A checker
  that always says "replace" gets screenshotted in a bad review.

### 3. Seasonal checklist

- **Examples:** "Before the first freeze: 9 things to check in a Dallas home", "The spring
  AC check you can do in 15 minutes", "After the storm: what to look for on your roof".
- **Format:** a one-page printable plus an opt-in for next season's reminder by text or
  email (that feeds the seasonal reminder in `/boring-marketing-email-sequences`).
- **Make it local:** name the climate and the housing stock (slab foundations, pier and
  beam, 1950s cast iron, attic installs). A generic checklist is what an AI answer gives
  for free.
- **Bridge:** split the list into "you can do this" and "call a pro for this". The second
  half is the tune-up booking.

### 4. Financing estimate

- **Inputs:** the project price (or the calculator's range) and the term.
- **Output:** an estimated monthly payment, with the lender's real terms and the note that
  approval depends on the lender.
- **Rules:** use the lender's published terms or the numbers the owner supplies, label it
  an estimate, and never promise approval. In the US, stating a monthly payment in an ad
  can trigger required credit disclosures (Regulation Z), so use the lender's approved
  wording.
- **Bridge:** "about $___ a month" turns a $9,000 replacement from "not this year" into
  "this month". It pairs with the price calculator.

## Other formats worth knowing

- **Photo quote.** "Text us a photo, get a price range back." Low effort for the customer,
  and the photo tells the tech what to bring.
- **The free inspection with a report.** The named method as a magnet: the camera video of
  the drain, the 40-photo roof report. Highest intent, highest cost to deliver.
- **"What does it cost in [city]?" guide pages.** Public, ungated price explainers with
  real ranges. They answer the question people ask search engines and assistants, and the
  call button is on the page.

## Example concepts (Hartline Plumbing, a sample business)

The owner wants more water heater replacements. Competitors offer "free estimates" and a
generic "plumbing tips" blog; none shows a price.

1. ★ **The Dallas Water Heater Price Check.** "What will a new water heater cost in your
   home? 30 seconds, no phone call." Calculator, ungated range, phone to book a slot.
   Bridge: the range builds trust, the written price and same-day slot win the job.
   ★ because no competitor posts a price and the top complaint in their reviews is
   surprise bills.
2. **Repair or Replace? The 60-Second Water Heater Check.** Age, symptom and quote in,
   recommendation out. Bridge: borderline and replace results book a free second opinion.
3. **The First-Freeze Checklist for Dallas Homes.** One page plus a reminder opt-in for next
   winter. Bridge: the "call a pro" half books pipe insulation and tune-ups.
