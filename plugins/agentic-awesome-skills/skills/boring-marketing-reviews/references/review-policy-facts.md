## Google: what's not allowed

From the Maps user contributed content policy, "Prohibited and restricted content",
under Fake engagement:

- "Contributions to Google Maps should reflect a genuine experience at a place or business. Fake engagement is not allowed and will be removed."
- Not allowed: to "Offer incentives", such as "payment, discounts, free goods and/or services", "in exchange for posting any review or revision or removal of a negative review."
- Not allowed: "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers." This is the rule that review gating breaks.
- "Merchants should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises, nor should they request that specific content be included."
- Not allowed: "Content that is based on a conflict of interest. A conflict of interest may include current or former employment, a contractual or consultory relationship, or other professional or personal affiliations." Staff, family, vendors and review swaps with other businesses fall here.

What is allowed, same page: "Solicit or encourage the posting of content that does represent a genuine experience, without offering incentives to do so or attempting to influence the rating or the contents of the review."

From "Tips to get more reviews" (Business Profile Help): "Offering incentives, like free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers to post reviews, change reviews, or remove negative reviews is considered fake & misleading content and is strictly prohibited."

## Google: what happens to a profile that breaks the rules

From "Business Profile restrictions for policy violations": Google may restrict a profile
so that it "will not be able to receive new reviews or ratings for set period of time",
its "existing reviews or ratings will be unpublished for set period of time", and it
"will display a warning to let consumers know that fake reviews were removed". Owners
are notified by email. Owners in the Business Profile community forum report 30-day
restrictions, including after review activity they didn't ask for, so a sudden burst of
reviews is worth avoiding even when it's genuine.

## Google: asking

- "To leave reviews, you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code." The
  link and QR code come from "Ask for reviews" on the Business Profile (in Google Search
  or the Maps app).
- "Customers must be signed into a Google Account to leave a review."

## Google: replying

From "Manage customer reviews" and "Tips to get more reviews":
- "Your reply is publicly posted" under the review, appearing as the business; the
  owner's personal name isn't shown.
- "The person who wrote the review is notified when you reply."
- Replies are reviewed against Google's policies, "usually" within 10 minutes, sometimes
  up to 30 days. Replies can be edited or deleted later.
- Google's tips: be "professional and polite", keep replies "clear and helpful", and for
  negative reviews, "Respond in a timely manner."
- A review that breaks policy can be reported for removal from the profile (Business
  Profile Help, "Report inappropriate reviews").

## Google: reviews and local ranking

From "Tips to improve your local ranking on Google": local results weigh relevance,
distance and prominence. On prominence: "This factor's also based on info like how many
websites link to your business and how many reviews you have." "More reviews and positive ratings can
help your business's local ranking." "There's no way to request or pay for a better local
ranking on Google."

## Google: review stars in search results

From the review snippet structured data guidelines (Google Search Central): when "the
entity that's being reviewed controls the reviews about itself", its pages using
`LocalBusiness` or other `Organization` markup "are ineligible for star review feature",
including reviews shown through an embedded Google or Facebook reviews widget. In force
since September 2019. Showing reviews on the site for people is fine; marking them up
for stars earns nothing.

## FTC (United States): the consumer reviews rule

Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465,
published August 22, 2024, in effect since October 21, 2024. Among other things it
prohibits selling or buying fake reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, certain
insiders writing reviews without clearly disclosing the relationship, and certain review
suppression.

- §465.4: it is a violation "for a business to provide compensation or other incentives in exchange for, or conditioned expressly or by implication on, the writing or creation of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment, whether positive or negative". The condition can be implied.
- §465.7: review suppression covers using unfounded legal threats, physical threats,
  intimidation or certain false public accusations to stop or remove a review, and
  claiming the reviews a business shows are all or most of its reviews when negative
  ones are being withheld for their rating or sentiment.

The rule is narrower than Google's policy on incentives (Google bans all incentives; the
FTC rule targets incentives tied to a sentiment). Follow Google's, which is stricter.
Outside the US, check local consumer law; the UK and EU have their own rules on fake and
incentivized reviews.

## What customers do with reviews

BrightLocal, Local Consumer Review Survey 2026 (1,002 US adults, published February 11,
2026). Self-reported survey answers; use them for direction, not forecasts.

- 74% look for reviews written in the last three months; 32% look for reviews from the
  last two weeks (20% the year before); 18% are only swayed by reviews from the last week.
- 31% will only use a business with 4.5 stars or more; 68% only with 4 or more.
- 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.
- 89% expect owners to respond to reviews; 81% expect a response within a week and 19%
  the same day.
- 83% of people asked to leave a review said they went on to leave one. Real ask
  programs see far lower rates; measure your own.
