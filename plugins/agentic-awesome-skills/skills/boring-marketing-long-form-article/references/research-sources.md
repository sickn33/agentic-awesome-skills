# Research: where the numbers come from, and how to cite them

Load at Phase 2. The ladder goes from most to least trusted. Use the highest rung that has the number.

## The ladder

1. **Government data.** US examples: Census County Business Patterns (businesses and employees by industry and county or metro, every year), the Economic Census (revenue by industry, every five years), the Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey (what businesses are doing now, including AI use), the Annual Business Survey (who owns businesses), BLS (wages by metro, employment projections), the SBA Office of Advocacy (small business profiles by state), the Federal Reserve's Small Business Credit Survey (what owners say their problems are). Other countries have equivalents (ONS, Statistics Canada, Eurostat).
2. **Large independent studies.** Pew Research Center, academic meta-analyses, large peer-reviewed samples. Say the sample when it matters.
3. **Industry associations.** Trade groups and their member surveys. Often useful, often small samples; say n.
4. **Vendor data.** A company's survey of its own customers or its own platform data. Fine to use, always labeled "(vendor data)", ideally with the sample size. Reviews platforms, call-tracking companies, email tools and ad tools all publish these.
5. **First-hand counts.** The author's own: "I checked 40 clinic sites in March." Real counts only, with the date and how they were counted. These are the most persuasive lines in an article and the easiest to fake, so every one is confirmed with the author.

## Citing

- In the article: plain names in parentheses, never links. "(Census, 2022 Economic Census)" · "(Pew Research, 2025)" · "(Acme, 2026 benchmark, vendor data)".
- In `sources.md`: the full trail. Source, title, date published, date accessed, URL, the exact figure as written, and the sample.
- Quote the figure the way the source states it. If the source says "may", the article doesn't say "will". If it says "up to", say "up to" or don't use it.

## Traps

- **Stat chains.** A blog cites a blog that cites a 2014 study. Follow it to the origin or drop it.
- **Old numbers presented as current.** Say the year.
- **The wrong place.** A global figure presented as US, or a national figure presented as one city.
- **Survey of the converted.** A vendor surveying its own customers about its own category.
- **Round-number suspicion.** "80% of customers" with no source is usually invented somewhere upstream.

## Useful industry codes (NAICS) for local-business pieces

Census tables are organized by these. Plumbing, heating and air conditioning contractors 238220 · electrical contractors 238210 · roofing contractors 238160 · offices of dentists 621210 · janitorial services 561720 · landscaping services 561730 · general automotive repair 811111.

## Reading time

Adults read non-fiction silently at about 238 words a minute (Brysbaert, 2019, a meta-analysis of reading-rate studies). Divide by 240 and round. A 4,300-word article is "about an 18 minute read".
