# Text-back and follow-up templates

Load in Phase 4 when writing the messages. Adapt to the voice profile when one exists.
Every message names the business and a person, says what happens next and when, and sells
nothing. Keep each under 300 characters so it lands as one text. Brackets are fields to
fill. Examples use the sample business Hartline Plumbing, Dallas TX.

## Missed call, business hours, no voicemail

> Hi, this is [Dana] at Hartline Plumbing. Sorry we missed your call, we're on a job. I'll
> call you back within [15 minutes]. If it's easier, text us here what's going on.

## Missed call, business hours, left a voicemail

> Thanks for the voicemail, this is [Dana] at Hartline Plumbing. I'm calling you back in
> the next [15 minutes]. If you can, text a photo of the problem so the tech knows what
> to bring.

## After hours, emergency trade with someone on call

> Hartline Plumbing here. If water is leaking now, call [on-call number] and our on-call
> tech will pick up. If it can wait, reply with your address and what's going on and we'll
> call at 7 am.

## After hours, no one on call

> Thanks for calling Hartline Plumbing. We're closed until [7 am]. Reply here with what you
> need and your address, and [Dana] will call you first thing. To shut off water now, the
> main valve is usually where the line enters the house.

The last line is optional. One useful thing in the first text earns trust; a coupon in
the first text reads as a robot.

## Web form or message acknowledgment

> Got your request, [first name]. This is [Dana] at Hartline Plumbing. I'll call you from
> this number within [10 minutes]. If now's a bad time, reply with a better one.

Then a human calls within the promised time. A promise missed is worse than no promise.

## Status texts that free the phone line

- **Booked:** "You're booked for [day], arrival between [10 and 12]. [Tech] will text when
  on the way."
- **On the way:** "[Tech] is on the way, about [25] minutes out."
- **Done:** "All done. Your invoice: [link]. Questions? Reply here."

## Quote follow-up

Day 0, right after the quote goes out:

> Sent your quote for the [water heater] to [email]. Happy to walk through it. Any
> questions, reply here.

Day 1, call. If no answer, leave a 20-second voicemail and send:

> Tried you about the [water heater] quote. Two options in there, the second one costs less
> up front. Call or text when it suits.

Day 3, something useful:

> Here's a photo of a [50-gallon] install we did in [neighborhood] last week, same setup as
> yours. [photo]

Day 7, call.

Day 14, close-out:

> Should I close out the [water heater] quote, or keep it open for you?

That question gets replies because it is easy to answer. A no is useful too: it ends the
chase and often comes with the reason (price, timing, went with someone else). Log the
reason. Patterns in the reasons belong in `/boring-marketing-performance-review`.
