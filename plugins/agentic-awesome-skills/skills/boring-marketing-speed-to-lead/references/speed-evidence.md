## Response time

- **Harvard Business Review, 2011** (Oldroyd, McElheran, Elkington, "The Short Life of
  Online Sales Leads"). An audit of how 2,241 US companies answered web leads, plus an
  analysis of 1.25 million leads at 42 companies. Average first response: 42 hours.
  Firms that tried to contact a lead within an hour were nearly 7 times as likely to
  qualify it as firms that tried an hour later, and more than 60 times as likely as firms
  that waited 24 hours or longer. Independent research on web leads at B2C and B2B
  companies.
- **Lead Response Management Study, 2007** (InsideSales.com with MIT researcher James
  Oldroyd) (vendor data). The odds of reaching a web lead fell about 100 times between a
  5-minute and a 30-minute first call, and the odds of qualifying it about 21 times.
  Web-form leads, outbound calls, B2B. Quote the direction; the multipliers came from
  that setting.

Neither study measured a homeowner with an emergency. For that case the logic is simpler:
the caller has a list of numbers and keeps dialing.

## How many calls go unanswered

- **Invoca, Home Services Lead Conversion Benchmarks Report, 2026** (vendor data). Across
  home services, 52% of callers spoke with a person; 65% for calls over 15 seconds, 73% for
  calls over 30 seconds. By trade: plumbing 74%, pest control 73%, carpeting 65%, home
  security 57%, lawn and trees 54%, doors and windows 53%, restoration 51%, HVAC 34%,
  construction 32%. Of calls a person answered, 38% were leads, and 45% of those leads
  converted on the call. The report also suggests pausing call ads during hours nobody can
  answer.
- **CallRail, "From Conversations to Conversions" benchmark, January 2025** (vendor data,
  read through secondary sources). Missed-call rate on 1.1 million leads from its own
  customers: healthcare 32%, legal 28%, home services 14%, real estate 9%. How a missed
  call is defined isn't published.
- **The figure not to use:** "85% of callers whose call goes unanswered won't call back."
  We found no study behind it. It traces to a phone vendor's blog post and keeps being
  repeated. Leave it out of every deliverable.

Answer rates differ by how each source defines "answered" (a person, any pickup, calls over
a duration). Compare a business with a benchmark by direction only.

## Texting rules (US)

- **A2P 10DLC:** business texting from a standard 10-digit local number has to be
  registered with The Campaign Registry through the phone provider. Major US carriers have
  blocked unregistered traffic since February 2025. Registration needs the legal business
  name, an EIN (sole proprietors have a separate path), sample messages and the opt-in
  wording. Toll-free numbers have their own verification.
- **Consent is separate from registration.** A registered number texting someone without
  the right consent is still exposed under the TCPA. A reply about the caller's own
  enquiry is the safer use; marketing texts need explicit consent. Not legal advice.

## Phone system features (checked 2026-11-13)

- **Quo (formerly OpenPhone):** auto-replies for missed calls, set per phone number in
  the web or desktop app, with four variants (missed with or without voicemail, during or
  after business hours). At most one auto-reply per contact every three hours. Texting
  requires carrier registration first. It also offers an AI agent (Sona) for answering and
  lead capture, priced separately.
- Other systems (RingCentral, Dialpad, Grasshopper, CallRail and field service tools such
  as Jobber or Housecall Pro) vary by plan. Check the current help docs for "missed call"
  or "auto-reply" before promising the feature.
