**How to use this list.** No single item proves AI authorship, since humans say "robust"
sometimes. The tell is **density and uniformity**: a human uses "delve" once a year; a
model uses it twice a paragraph, in prose where every paragraph is also the same length
and every list has three items. When building a profile's AVOID section, pull the 10-20
entries most likely to contaminate THIS brand's content (a dev-tools brand needs the
"seamless/robust" cluster; a coach needs the "journey/unlock" cluster). When
drift-checking, flag any cluster of 3+ tells in one piece.

## Word tells

The 2023-26 canon: individually survivable, lethal in clusters:

- delve, tapestry, testament, beacon, realm, landscape (metaphorical), navigate
  (metaphorical), journey (metaphorical)
- robust, seamless, comprehensive, holistic, pivotal, crucial, vital, invaluable
- leverage (as verb), utilize, harness, foster, bolster, underscore, streamline,
  optimize (reflexive use)
- elevate, unlock, unleash, empower, supercharge, revolutionize, transform (reflexive
  use)
- vibrant, bustling, intricate, nuanced, multifaceted, dynamic, ever-evolving,
  fast-paced
- game-changer, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, next-level, world-class, best-in-class
- plethora, myriad, treasure trove, wealth of, array of, a host of
- embark, embarking, dive (as in "let's dive in"), deep dive, unpack, explore (as
  section verb)
- moreover, furthermore, additionally, notably, importantly (as paragraph openers)
- akin to, albeit, whilst, thereby, thus (in casual registers where the human never
  writes this way)
- synergy, alignment, ecosystem, paradigm, framework (when nothing is actually a
  framework)
- meticulous, showcase, facilitate, endeavor, encompass, spearhead, catalyze, resonate,
  profound, unprecedented, compelling, scalable, enhance, innovative

2026 additions, the "quiet register" that replaced the buzzword set once models were
tuned away from it (reported Feb-May 2026; each is fine once, lethal as a house style):

- quietly / quiet as intensifier ("quiet confidence", "quietly powerful", "quietly
  growing"), shift (noun), matters, shape (verb), land (verb), hold, pull, compound
  (verb: "small actions compound"), signal (noun), earn ("earn trust", "earn the right
  to"), "the work", actually / real as sincerity markers
- "built different" / "built for the bold" / "built to last" (stock LLM positioning)

## Phrase tells

- "It's not just X, it's Y" / "This isn't about X. It's about Y." (the signature
  2024-26 construction)
- "In today's [fast-paced/digital/ever-changing] world/landscape..."
- "Whether you're a [X] or a [Y]..." (false-inclusive opener)
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's get started" / "Buckle up"
- "The best part?" / "Here's the kicker:" / "But here's the thing:" (fine once; models
  use one per section)
- "Enter [product name]." (dramatic-pivot introduction)
- "Think of it as [analogy]" (reflexive analogy-making)
- "From [A] to [B], ..." (sweeping false-range: "From startups to Fortune 500s...")
- "Look no further" / "...has you covered" / "without breaking the bank" / "a win-win"
- "Take your X to the next level" / "elevate your X game" / "the secret sauce"
- "more than just a [category]" / "at its core" / "at the end of the day"
- "It's worth noting that" / "It's important to note/remember that" / "Needless to say"
- "When it comes to X..." (empty topic-shifter)
- "In conclusion" / "Ultimately," / "The bottom line?" (formulaic wrap-ups)
- "Great question!" / "I hope this helps!" / "Let me know if you have any questions!" /
  "Happy to help!" / "Best of luck on your journey!" / "As an AI..." (assistant-register
  leakage)
- "Here's the part most people miss" / "I'm going to state this as clearly as possible"
  / "Let me be clear:" (2026 openers that promise directness, then deliver the standard
  advice)
- "Studies have shown" / "Research suggests" with no study named
- "a double-edged sword" / "That being said," / "The key takeaway is" / "As the world
  continues to evolve..."
- Rhetorical question immediately self-answered: "So what does this mean for you? It
  means..."
- "no fluff, no filler" / "actionable insights" / "battle-tested" (anti-AI phrases that
  became AI phrases)

## Structure tells

- **Rule-of-three runs**: adjective triplets ("fast, reliable, and secure"),
  three-example lists, three-part parallel clauses, repeated until the prose has a
  drumbeat. Humans vary list lengths.
- **Em-dash chains** are model-dependent since 2026. The Economist's 56k-sentence comparison
  found ChatGPT now uses fewer em-dashes than humans; only Claude still over-uses them. A
  chain is still a tell in a voice that never used them; absence proves nothing.
- **Thin punctuation, long sentences** (the 2026 replacement tell): few commas or
  semicolons, almost no parentheses, clauses chained with "and", the most over-used word
  in model prose, plus Latin/Greek-root polysyllables where a plain word would do.
- **Nobody quoted**: claims attributed to no one; no expert, customer, or source named.
- "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." staccato denial pattern.
- Bolded-phrase-colon bullets everywhere: "**Speed:** it's fast. **Trust:** it's
  trusted."
- Perfectly parallel bullet grammar across an entire list (humans drift mid-list).
- Every section closing on a one-line zinger or mic-drop fragment.
- Question-form headers answered in the first sentence below ("Why does this matter?
  Because...").
- Colon-titles as default: "X: Why Y Matters More Than Ever."
- Summary paragraphs that restate the section just read; conclusions that restate the
  intro.
- Hedge stacks: "may potentially," "could possibly help," "in some cases might",
  multiple qualifiers on one claim.
- Both-sides-ism: every opinion immediately balanced ("That said, ...", "However, it's
  important to consider...").
- Sycophantic or throat-clearing openers before the actual point.
- Emoji as section markers (🚀 Growth, 💡 Insight) in registers where the human never does
  this.

## Rhythm & cadence tells

- **Uniform paragraph length**: every paragraph 2-3 sentences, metronomic. Human writing
  has a 1-sentence jab next to a 6-sentence ramble.
- **Uniform sentence length**: everything 15-28 words ("low burstiness"). No genuinely long sentence that
  risks losing the thread, no 2-word fragment placed for impact rather than pattern.
- Fragment-for-emphasis deployed on schedule ("Every. Single. Time.") rather than
  rarely.
- Antithesis cadence overuse: "less about X, more about Y" / "It's not about working
  harder, it's about working smarter."
- Escalation triads: "good → great → exceptional" ladders.
- Every paragraph opening with a discourse marker (But / And / So / Yet / Still). The
  marker itself is human; 100% coverage is not.
- No digressions, asides, half-finished thoughts, or self-corrections. Real voices
  wander once in a while; model prose never does.
- Tonal flatline: identical energy from first sentence to last. Humans heat up and cool
  down.
- Closing with an inspirational call-forward ("The future of X is bright, and it starts
  with you.").

## What humans do that models don't (use these as positive checks in Drift Check)

- Inconsistent contraction use, idiosyncratic punctuation habits, a pet phrase used
  slightly wrong
- Specific sensory or autobiographical detail that serves no persuasive purpose
- Numbers that are oddly precise or oddly rounded, not template-clean
- Opinions stated without a balancing clause
- References to their own earlier content, running jokes, callbacks
