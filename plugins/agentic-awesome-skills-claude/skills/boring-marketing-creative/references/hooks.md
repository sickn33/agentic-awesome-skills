# Hook bank

The openings that live ads actually use, taken from 336 live US Meta ads from 48 DTC brands
(pulled 2026-11-16, every ad viewed). Each pattern has the ad it was seen in, when it fits, and
fill-in examples for other kinds of business. Pair this file with `ad-formats.md`: the format is
the container, the hook is the first second inside it.

A hook is what the viewer sees and reads in the first second with the sound off. For a still, it
is where the eye lands. For a video, it is the first frame plus the first caption.

## What the openings had in common

- **Something real in frame at once.** 46 of the 94 videos we could watch frame by frame open on
  a face to camera and 18 on hands holding the product. 68 of those 94 show the product within
  2 seconds; 192 of 197 stills show it at once.
- **The caption carries the hook.** 21 of the 46 face-to-camera openings caption the spoken
  words, mostly 1 to 4 words at a time. About a quarter of all 139 videos (34) open on a caption
  that stops mid-sentence.
- **A few borrow the feed's own furniture:** a reply-to-comment bubble (4 videos), "POV" (3), a
  search bar, an iMessage thread, an Instagram link sticker.
- **A few words on screen.** Most openings we viewed put one short line on screen. Long text
  blocks do appear, but as native captions over phone footage (Huel, 57 days), not as a layout.

## The patterns

Spoken hooks come from a real person: the owner, a staff member, a customer or a paid creator who
used the product. An AI-generated presenter may say a myth, a tip, a question or a POV line; it never
says a confession, a result or anything else in the first person as a customer.

### 1. Confession: "I was about to..."
- **Seen:** Atlas Coffee Club, 572 days, the oldest ad in the pull. A woman holds a Keurig over the
  trash, captioned "I was about to throw my Keurig away"
  ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=672977635689815)).
- **Fits:** the product replaces something the viewer already owns, pays for or has given up on.
- **Fill-ins:** a homeowner: "I was about to replace the whole water heater." A dispatcher at a
  software customer: "I was about to hire a second dispatcher." A tax client: "I was about to file
  another extension." A coffee drinker: "I was about to cancel my subscription."
- **Rule:** a real person telling their own story. Never an AI person, never a scripted stranger
  presented as a customer.

### 2. Myth or tip: "I never put X in Y"
- **Seen:** Atlas Coffee Club, 396 days: "I never put oily beans in my espresso machine" over a
  macro of beans ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1397865848579594)). Atlas, 377 days:
  "My barista told me this coffee secret" over a hand holding a portafilter
  ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=3194950073979529)).
- **Fits:** anything with a wrong way to do it and an expert who knows the right one.
- **Fill-ins:** plumber: "I never pour drain cleaner down a slow drain." Dentist: "I never brush
  right after coffee." Bookkeeper: "I never let a client pay estimated taxes from checking."
  Scheduling software: "I never send a crew out without the gate code in the job."

### 3. Identity or symptom callout: "If you're..., you need..."
- **Seen:** Cozy Earth, 131 days, a hand-drawn whiteboard: "If you're waking up drenched, you need
  these" ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1887019256070526)). PetLab Co, 200 days, a vet:
  "If your dog eats grass..." ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1519997246498037)).
- **Fits:** a situation the buyer recognizes in themselves in one line.
- **Fill-ins:** roofer: "If your ceiling has a brown ring, look up." Accountant: "If you paid an
  underpayment penalty last April..." Field-service software: "If your techs still call the office
  for the next address..."
- **Rule:** name the situation, never a personal attribute. Meta rejects "Are you overweight?" and
  accepts "Waking up drenched?"

### 4. Reverse psychology: "...ignore this"
- **Seen:** Cozy Earth, 243 days, a creator carousel: "If you're a girl who hates being cozy..."
  with "IGNORE THIS" over a couple photo ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1665168554828729)).
- **Fits:** comfort, taste and convenience products, and brands with a light voice.
- **Fill-ins:** HVAC: "If you like a 58 degree bedroom, scroll past." Invoicing software: "If you
  enjoy chasing invoices, ignore this." Bakery: "If you hate warm bread, keep scrolling."

### 5. Question
- **Seen:** Lemme, 111 days: "Want brunch without the bloat?" over a hand holding the bottle and a
  plate of eggs ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1546668280292013)). Trade Coffee,
  51 days, a search bar: "How do I find new coffees that I'll actually like?"
  ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1612586367073535)).
- **Fits:** the buyer already asks this question. Pull it from reviews, sales calls and search
  terms rather than inventing it.
- **Fill-ins:** "Is your water heater older than your car?" "Still building the schedule on a
  whiteboard?" "Want your books closed by the 5th?"

### 6. Timely or cultural
- **Seen:** Huel, 178 days: "GLP-1 killed your appetite. Not your nutritional needs."
  ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1169896608601745)). Atlas Coffee Club, 69 days: "Me
  preparing for a Netflix documentary about how coffee is really made"
  ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=3470897453084064)).
- **Fits:** a change your buyer is living through right now: a drug, a law, a season, a show.
- **Fill-ins:** HVAC: "First freeze is Thursday. Is your furnace ready?" Tax practice: "Tax
  season starts in six weeks. Here's the one form to find now." Scheduling software: "Busy season
  starts in 30 days. Is your schedule ready for it?"
- **Rule:** dated hooks expire. Note the end date in the matrix and retire the ad on it.

### 7. Result quote
- **Seen:** Huel, 247 days: "Lost 80lbs (so far) with Huel." with the reviewer's first name, the
  year and Trustpilot stars ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=4239632006288183)).
  The same quote ran in at least three designs (two at 247 days, one at 162).
- **Fits:** you hold real reviews that name a specific result.
- **Fill-ins:** "'Fixed in one visit, and the price matched the quote.' Maria R., Google review."
  "'We stopped double-booking crews the first week.' Ops manager, 14-truck HVAC company."
  "'First year we didn't owe a penalty.' Dana K., client since 2023."
- **Rule:** verbatim, from a real reviewer, with permission for anything beyond the public post.
  A weight, health or money result needs a typical-results note or a different review.

### 8. Velocity stat
- **Seen:** PetLab Co, 149 days: "One tub sold every 12 seconds" in script type beside the tub
  ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1051864764181176)).
- **Fits:** you can count it and the number is big for your size.
- **Fill-ins:** "3,200 water heaters installed in Dallas since 2015." "A crew schedule gets built
  on our app every 4 seconds." "Sold out by 10am on 41 Saturdays this year."
- **Rule:** counted, current and checkable. Keep the source in the campaign notes.

### 9. Dare
- **Seen:** Atlas Coffee Club, 312 days: "We dare you to drink our espresso black..." over a single
  shot glass ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=732747836141467)).
- **Fits:** quality you can let the buyer test, ideally with a guarantee behind it.
- **Fill-ins:** "Get three quotes. We dare you to find one in writing that beats ours." "Try to
  break the schedule. We dare you." "One class. We dare you not to come back."

### 10. POV
- **Seen:** True Classic, 77 days: "POV: asking neighbors for eggs", a creator's phone video at a
  front door ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2067350817236397)). Hungryroot, 202 days:
  a POV caption about outsourcing the grocery run over a man unpacking groceries
  ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=4309638865973296)).
- **Fits:** a small everyday moment the product changes. Shot from the viewer's eyes or the
  creator's phone.
- **Fill-ins:** "POV: your AC quits at 2pm in July." "POV: it's month-end and the books are already
  closed." "POV: you open the fridge and dinner's handled."

### 11. Reply to a comment
- **Seen:** MUD\WTR, 41 days: a staff member answers a comment bubble that says the product is
  expensive, with word-by-word captions ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2312904466137170)).
  Our Place, 46 days: "What do pro chefs use to always get the perfect sear?!" over a steak
  searing in the pan ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2596075900855943)).
- **Fits:** a real objection or question people keep asking. Price objections work well because
  the answer is the pitch.
- **Fill-ins:** a plumber answering "Why do you charge just to show up?" Software founder answering
  "Does it work without signal?" Dentist answering "Does whitening hurt?"
- **Rule:** reply to a real comment, shown as it was written. Blur the commenter's name and photo
  unless they agree.

### 12. The mid-sentence caption
- **Seen:** Trade Coffee, 109 days: the first caption reads "Delicious coffee and it" over a creator
  holding the bag to camera ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1580079020173490)). Graza,
  84 days: "We grew eleven million pounds of potatoes" as the founder starts talking
  ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1711390340123334)). Cometeer, 133 days: "This right
  here is" as the founder pushes a frozen tray at the lens
  ([ad](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1537572474773598)).
- **Fits:** any talking video. Start the cut mid-thought, so the first caption is already moving.
- **How:** cut the first half-second of breath and set-up. Captions 1 to 4 words at a time, the
  spoken word marked.

## Making hook variants

- **For a UGC or creator video, film 3 openings and one body.** The person says three different
  first lines, then the same demo. Cut three versions that differ only in the first 2 to 3 seconds.
- **For a still, change the line and keep the picture**, or keep the line and change the picture.
  Never both in one test.
- **Mix the patterns.** One batch gets at least three different patterns from this list, not three
  wordings of one.
- **Before using a hook, check it.** Would you stop for it at 11pm while tired? Could it run for ten
  other products? (Then it is too generic.) Can you deliver what it promises? Is it as short as it
  can be? Does it sound like the business?
