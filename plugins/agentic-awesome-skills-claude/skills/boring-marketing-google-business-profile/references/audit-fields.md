# Audit fields: where to read them and what good looks like

Load before Phase 2 of /boring-marketing-google-business-profile. "Public" means anyone can read it on the profile in Google Search or Maps. "Owner" means it needs the profile editor (manager access or screenshots). Record unobserved fields as unobserved, never as missing.

| Field | Where | What good looks like | How to compare with the top 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name | Public | Matches the sign and the website exactly | Note any winner with keywords or a city in its name (rule-breaking, reportable) |
| Primary category | Public (shown under the name) | The most specific category for the main service | The single most important match. If all three winners share one and the business doesn't, that's gap 1 |
| Secondary categories | Owner; for competitors, free browser extensions | A few that describe real lines of business | List categories two or more winners share that the business lacks |
| Services | Owner (editor); partly public | Each real service listed, with a plain description; custom services for what the predefined list misses | Services winners list that the business does but hasn't listed |
| Description | Public | Plain words: what they do, where, since when, what makes them the call. No keyword lists | Rarely decisive. Fix last unless it's misleading |
| Hours and holiday hours | Public | True, including after-hours as the owner confirms it | A winner showing "Open 24 hours" for an emergency trade wins urgent calls the business can't take if it closes at 5 |
| Phone numbers | Public and owner | Every number reaches someone and is tracked | Not compared; a risk check |
| Website link and its landing page | Public | Tracking parameters; lands on a page that matches the main service and city | Note whether winners link to a service or city page rather than the homepage |
| Booking, ordering, messaging buttons | Public | Each one works and someone answers | Note who offers messaging or booking |
| Rating and review count | Public | Steady, recent, real | Count, rating, and the gap in reviews |
| Review pace | Public (sort by newest; may require sign-in) | Reviews arriving every week or two | Count reviews dated in the last 90 days for each profile, then reviews per month |
| Reply share | Public | Owner replies on most recent reviews, the bad ones especially | Share of the last 20 reviews with an owner reply |
| Photos | Public | Real job photos added regularly; logo and cover set | Rough count and the date of the newest owner photo |
| Posts | Public (Updates); views in the owner's Posts tab | One recent post that would help a customer decide | Date of each winner's newest post |
| Attributes | Public, partly | The ones that matter to buyers in this trade (online estimates, on-site service, identity attributes if true) | Attributes winners show that are true for the business |
| Service areas | Owner | Up to 20 real areas, within about 2 hours' drive | Not compared |
| Distance | Public (map) | Not changeable | Distance from each search point, for the honest read of what's reachable |
| Performance | Owner | Calls, website clicks and direction requests trending up; search terms that match the services | Owner only. Use the search terms list to see which queries already show the profile |
| Suggested edits and Collected info | Owner | Notifications read within the 4-day window; nothing wrong left standing | Not compared; a risk check |

## Reading review pace without fooling yourself

- Use dates, not the total count. A winner with 900 reviews and 3 in the last quarter is coasting; one with 140 and 25 last quarter is pulling away.
- Sorting by newest may require a signed-in account. If you can't sort, say the pace is unobserved rather than estimating it from the first page shown.
- Never suggest buying, trading or filtering reviews. Gaps in review pace go to /boring-marketing-reviews, which sets up an honest ask after every job.

## Ranking the gap list

Order: risks, relevance, prominence, conversion, habits (as in the skill). Within a tier, put first what changes fastest for least effort. Write each gap in one line:

`Gap · what we found against the top 3 · the fix · effort (minutes, hours, weeks) · Google guidance or practitioner experience, in plain words`

Example (sample business, illustrative values):

`Primary category · "Contractor" while all three use "Plumber" · switch to "Plumber", expect re-verification · 10 minutes plus verification · Google guidance (specific primary category)`

`Review pace · 2 a month against 7, 9 and 12 · a review ask after every completed job, via /boring-marketing-reviews · two weeks to set up · Google guidance (reviews count toward prominence)`
